# Exchange

The Cartesian exchange matrix `K_ab = Σ_cd D_cd (ac|bd)` of an AO density, by
McMurchie–Davidson, on the GPU, from kernels generated by
[FelizJueves](../../../../../deps/FelizJueves) (`Target.K`, `kernel_kblock.py`): one
warp per contracted shell block `K_ab`, opt-UM's mapping (Palethorpe, Stocks &
Barca, arXiv:2407.21445, §4.3). **Screened** at `tau` (default `1e-13`) by
the dressed Schwarz bound and the density's **decay with distance** (§4.3.1):
a block's warp stops its bra loop at the first bra with
`Ip · Iq[ket0] · F_ab < tau`, and each lane its ket loop at its first ket with
`Ip · Iq · F_ab < tau`, `F_ab` the block's density factor, a bound on every
`|D_cd|` it can visit. Every skipped term is under `tau`.

## Module

`free_breakfast.integrals.eri.integral_direct.exchange`, also re-exported by
`free_breakfast.integrals`. It re-exports
[`free_breakfast.integrals.eri.integral_direct.exchange.pairs`](pairs/README.md),
so the one import gives both the ordered classes the build reads and the build:

```cpp
auto mmd = primitive_pair_classes(res, basis);              // once per basis
auto classes = ordered_pair_classes(res, basis, *mmd);      // once per basis
FBF_TRY(exchange(res, *classes, density, k));               // per density
```

The classes are a **set**: each bra's block tables are made against every
class of its `ordered_pair_classes` call, so `exchange` takes the whole set in
its order and refuses a subset or a reordering. They also hold the build's
scratch, so one build at a time per set (see "Scratch" below).

## Design

FelizJueves's `kb_driver` (`test/kblock_driver.py`) and `launch_kblock.py` are
the launch contract. K has no symmetry inside a pair, so its bra `(a, c)` and
ket `(b, d)` are **ordered** shell pairs, the first shell of each an output
index: every [ordered class](pairs/README.md) is both a bra class and a ket
class, and one generated kernel per (bra class, ket class) adds it into `K`:
`kb<la, lc, lb, ld>` (`kernels/kb/`, dispatched as `kb_kernels[la][lc][lb][ld]`)
when every shell is at most d, and FelizJueves's rolled
`kbr<la, lc, lb, ld>` (`kernels/kbr/`, `kbr_kernels`) when any shell is f,
FelizJueves's rule "through d kb, at f kbr" (#580). `kbr` is `kb`'s mapping,
screen and output with two changes: `E` is an input, each record's undressed
`E^{la lb}` (nonzero entries, record-major; the ket's odd-`|t|` entries
negated) made once per basis by `exchange_e_table` into the ordered classes'
`e_bra` and `e_ket`, only for a basis with an f shell; and its contraction is
loops over five class tables (the two `E` patterns and `hpq`, compiled in as
device arrays, `kernels/kbr/kbr_tables.h`), so its text does not grow with the
class. A `kb` at f is never emitted: `kb_33_33` is 22 MB of source and never
left cicc (FelizJueves #569). K's limits are `kExchangeMaxMomentum` (f) and
`kExchangeKbMaxMomentum` (d) in `helpers/exchange_helpers_bridge.h`, which
host units read and `exchange_launch.cu` checks against the two tables. Each record's
Schwarz bound is the ordered classes' `record_bound`, FelizJueves's **dressed**
`ao.k.record_bounds`, formed once per basis ([`pairs/`](pairs/README.md)); `kb`
reads it as `Ip` on the bra and `Iq` on the ket, and the reach and `G_max`
below read it too. `mmd`'s undressed `bound`, which J reads, is not used here.

**One warp per `K_ab`.** A `kb` launch takes a **block table**: per block, a
range of bra records of one first shell `a` (every `(a, c)` record of the
class), a range of ket records of one first shell `b` (every `(b, d)`), and
the AO offsets of `a` and `b`. Every lane walks the block's bras, the lanes
take its kets, each lane keeps its own `K_ab` accumulator, and after one warp
reduction lane 0 adds the block into `K` itself. So there is no per-record
scratch and no scatter, and a launch's blocks never write the same element.
The ranges are the classes' groups as they are: each class is grouped by first
shell, the groups in basis order, each sorted non-increasing in `record_bound`
once per basis, which is the order both of `kb`'s `break`s need. So the pairs'
layout is the one `kernel_k` used.

`exchange(res, classes, density, k)` runs:

1. On `res.stream()`: zero `K`, then `max|D|` (`exchange_density_max`,
   `helpers/`, the decay's first field), and zero the bound maximum.
2. **The rank tables and centres**, each class on its own stream: its largest
   record bound folded into one `G_max` (`exchange_bound_max`); and from a
   class `(l, l)`, the AO offset of every shell of momentum `l` by rank, read
   off its records' `rank_b` and `off_b` (`exchange_rank_offsets`), and every
   one of its AOs' centres (`exchange_ao_centres`). A class `(l, l)` names
   every shell of `l`, each paired with itself. The join closes the phase:
   every gather reads two tables, the decay every centre, every geometry
   `G_max`. Then, on `res.stream()`, **the density's decay** (`Λ`, `H_off`,
   below) from the centres (`exchange_density_decay`), and the first AO of
   every AO's shell, from the tables (`exchange_shell_starts`).
3. **The density blocks and geometry**, each class `(lc, ld)` on its own
   stream: `Dcd` of `(lc, ld)`, gathered from `D` (`exchange_gather_dcd`), and
   the class's group geometry, each first shell's centre and **reach**
   (`exchange_group_geometry`). Every `(lc, ld)` of two reached momenta is a
   reached class, so each `Dcd` has a stream. The join closes the phase: every
   bra class reads them.
4. **Every class as a bra**, each on its own stream: for every ket class, its
   block table's density factors (`exchange_factors`) and one `kb` launch over
   the table into `K`, in that order.
5. After the join, on `res.stream()`: every `a > b` block of `K` set to its
   transpose, negated for an antisymmetric density (`exchange_mirror`).

**The block tables are made once per basis**, on the device, by
`ordered_pair_classes` ([`pairs/`](pairs/README.md)): each class carries its
groups' bounds on the device (`group_start`), and `exchange_blocks` writes one
launch's table from a bra class's and a ket class's, one thread block per bra
group. The host knows the basis too, so it counts each table's blocks itself
(the grid and the allocation, each class's `n_blk`) and nothing is read back.
The table depends only on the basis; each block's density factor depends on
the density, so `exchange_factors` rewrites it beside the table per call, the
same walk over the groups as the table's so the two agree on the order.

**The density factor** is FelizJueves's `ao.k.density_decay` (#484), the
paper's eqs. 41–49 and its Figure 3's three exits made a guaranteed bound, and
its module docstring has the proof. Per call, at the screen's `tau`, with
`G_max` the largest record bound:

| | |
|---|---|
| `R` | a group's **reach**: the largest `\|AC\|` over its records `(a, c)` with `record_bound · (G_max · max\|D\|) >= tau (1 - 2^-40)`, or none. A record under that cutoff is never visited under any factor at most `max\|D\|` (its group's sorted `break` fires first), so the cutoff is the screen's own: `mmd` screens no pairs, and over all of a group's records `R` would be the farthest shell in the molecule (the paper's eq. 42 prints `<`, read as `>=`) |
| `Λ` | the least-squares slope of `log(max \|D_mn\|` per 1-bohr bin of `\|MN\|)` over the **off-centre** elements, negated and clamped to `>= 0` |
| `H_off` | `max \|D_mn\| e^{Λ\|MN\|}` over the off-centre elements: an exact reduction, not the paper's regression intercept, so `\|D_mn\| <= H_off e^{-Λ\|MN\|}` holds at every one whatever the fit |
| `F_ab` | `max\|D\|` when `d = \|AB\| - R_a - R_b <= 2 · 10^-6` bohr or a reach is absent, else `min(max\|D\|, H_off e^{-Λ d} (1 + 2^-40))` |

A visited bra `(a, c)` has `|AC| <= R_a`, and a ket `(b, d)` `|BD| <= R_b`, so
`|CD| >= d`: past `d > 0` every visited `(c, d)` is off-centre (the same-atom
element, which the paper's Figure 4 shows above its fitted line, never enters
such a block) and `|D_cd| <= H_off e^{-Λ d}`. With the **dressed** record bound
(`ao.k.record_bounds`) as `Ip` and `Iq`, which is what `kb` reads, **every
skipped term is under `tau`** either way (the paper's looser `τ* = τ/N*` is not
taken). Because the dressed bound carries the pair factor, it decays with
`|AC|`, so a reach stops short of the molecule and the factor fires: on the H₁₀
chain (23 bohr) **24 of the 1,860** `a <= b` block factors fall below `max|D|`
at the default `tau = 1e-13`, 72 at `1e-10` (half the 144 FelizJueves's own
`density_decay` gives over every block: a block's factor is its transpose's,
and no `a == b` block is under `max|D|`) and 121 at `1e-6`; the smallest is
0.0136 against `max|D|` 0.328. On H₂O every factor is `max|D|`.

**Why the default is `1e-13`, not J's `1e-10`.** The guarantee is per skipped
term; `K_ab` sums many of them, and their number grows with the record pairs
covering the element. With the dressed bound, which is mostly well under
`mmd`'s undressed one, the screen skips far more than it did, and the sums
show (RX 9060 XT, worst `|K - vk|` as a fraction of `max|K|`, against the
`1e-12` bar):

| `tau` | H₂O/STO-3G | H₂O/cc-pVDZ | H₁₀ chain |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 6.2e-16 | 3.3e-15 | 3.6e-15 |
| 1e-10 | 1.1e-11 | 6.9e-10 | 7.6e-10 |
| 1e-11 | 1.5e-13 | 3.4e-11 | 8.3e-11 |
| 3e-12 | 3.5e-13 | 4.0e-12 | 1.6e-11 |
| 1e-12 | 1.7e-14 | 1.2e-12 | 5.9e-12 |
| 3e-13 | 1.7e-14 | 3.3e-13 | 2.0e-12 |
| **1e-13** | 1.2e-14 | 7.3e-14 | 7.5e-13 |
| 1e-14 | 7.8e-16 | 3.5e-15 | 5.2e-14 |

FelizJueves's own host screened `K` (`ao.k.exchange_matrix_screened`) misses
STO-3G's and cc-pVDZ's `vk` at `1e-10` by the same 6.2e-11 and 3.2e-9, so the
error is the bound's, not this build's. `1e-13` is the largest round `tau` at
which all three meet the bar; the chain, the tightest, keeps 25% of it. A
longer molecule covers each element with more record pairs, so it may need a
smaller `tau` still. Two centres closer than `10^-6` bohr are
**one centre** here (`kExchangeSameCentre`): a centre is recomputed from a
record as `P - (P - A)`, which can miss `A` by an ulp, and FelizJueves's exact
equality would split an atom's AOs; hence the `2 · 10^-6` cut on `d` where
FelizJueves's is 0. `2^-40` is rounding room that only loosens the bound. A
molecule with no visible decay gets `Λ = 0` and `F_ab = max|D|`, the screen
before this one exactly.

**The bra-ket swap.** Renaming `c <-> d` and using `(ac|bd) = (bd|ac)` gives
`K[D]^T = K[D^T]`. So a symmetric `D` has a symmetric `K`, `K_ba = K_ab^T`,
and an antisymmetric `D` an antisymmetric one, `K_ba = -K_ab^T`: either way
only the shell blocks `a <= b` need computing. The tables hold only those, cut
on the device by AO offset (the groups are in basis order, so offsets rise
with them; the host's count compares shell indices, the same order), and
`exchange_mirror` then sets each element of an `a > b` block from its
transpose, times `-1` for `DensitySymmetry::Antisymmetric`. An `a == b` block
is computed whole and left as it is. What is left of the duplication is
inside the `a == b` blocks: `O(n_shells)` of `O(n_shells^2)`. The paper's K
does the same: it uses only this symmetry (§4.3.1), and FelizJueves's
`ao/k.py` "THE TWO SWEEPS" has the identity and the unique-sweep reference.

**A general `D` is two sweeps.** `D = S + A`, `S = (D + D^T)/2` and
`A = (D - D^T)/2` (one `geam`): `exchange` on `S` as `Symmetric` plus
`exchange` on `A` as `Antisymmetric` is `K[D]`, at the same `kb` work as one
sweep over every block, plus the `geam` and two mirrors; and each half fits
its own decay, so it screens at least as tightly. There is no every-block
sweep and no general-`D` wrapper, since nothing in the tree has a
non-symmetric `D` yet. **Codes usually have the two parts already:** TDHF/RPA
works with `X + Y` and `X − Y`, which are exactly the symmetric and the
antisymmetric parts; so do CIS transition densities, and the response to an
imaginary perturbation (a magnetic field gives an antisymmetric density).

**The symmetry is a contract, not a check.** `exchange` does not verify it:
refusing would cost a device pass over `D` *and* a host synchronization to
read the verdict back into the `Status`, where the build is otherwise fully
asynchronous. Handed a `D` of neither symmetry, it returns its `K`'s `a <= b`
blocks and the mirror of them that `DensitySymmetry` names.

What the kernels leave to the launcher, and how it is supplied:

| Input | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| bra fields | `p_*`, `zeta`, `pa_*`, `pb_*`, `s` of the bra class as `p_*`, `zeta_ac`, `pa_*`, `pc_*`, `s_ac` | "a" is a record's first shell, "b" (here `c`) its second |
| ket fields | the ket class's same fields as `q_*`, `eta`, `qb_*`, `qd_*`, `s_bd`, `Iq` = `record_bound`, `dd` = `rank_b` | the ket's first shell is `b`, its second `d` |
| `Ip` | the bra class's `record_bound` | the record's own dressed Schwarz bound; the density is in `dmax` |
| `dc`, `dd` | `dc = rank_b · n_shells(ld)` per (bra class, `ld`), the class's `dc` (`ordered_pair_bra_rows`, once per basis), `dd = rank_b` | the gather's `Dcd` row is `rank_c · n_shells(ld) + rank_d`; `dc` depends on the ket's `ld`, so a bra class has one row set per `ld` |
| `n_dc` | `n_shells(lc) · n_shells(ld)` | an extent only, never read |
| `bra0` … `ko_b` | the bra class's block table against the ket class, one segment of `n_blk` per field (`exchange_blocks`, once per basis) | a block's bras and kets are one group each, so each range is non-increasing in `record_bound`: both screens are `break`s, and unsorted ranges would make them a wrong answer, not a slow one |
| `dmax` | each block's `F_ab`, `n_blk` doubles beside the table (`exchange_factors`, per call) | constant within a block, so the sorted `break`s stay exact |
| `K`, `n_ao` | `K` itself, zeroed | lane 0 `atomicAdd`s the reduced block at `(ko_a, ko_b)`: no two blocks of a launch meet, and launches of several class pairs, from several streams, need no ordering |
| the launch | grid `n_blk`, block `kb_block_dim = {1, FJ_WAVEFRONT}`, **no dynamic shared memory** | `kb`'s `Ksh[(n_p + n_a n_b) · FJ_WAVEFRONT]` is static, sized for exactly that block |
| `kbr` only: `Eac`, `Ebd` | the bra class's `e_bra`, the ket class's `e_ket` | in place of `pa_*` … `qd_*`, which `kbr` does not read |
| `kbr` only: `ac_*`, `bd_*`, `hpq` | `kbr_e_ptr` / `kbr_e_col` at the bra pair's and the ket pair's offsets, `kbr_hpq` at `(la + lc, lb + ld)`'s (`kbr_tables.h`, by `wwrGetSymbolAddress`) | constants of the class, written by the emitter from FelizJueves's `tables(am)` |

**The shared-memory budget is checked at compile time.** `Ksh` is static, so
`exchange_launch.cu` `static_assert`s the formula
`(n_p + n_a n_b) · kb_block_dim[1] · 8` bytes, `n_p = (lp+1)(lp+2)(lp+3)/6`,
`lp = la + lc`, at the tables' largest class, `(ff|ff)`, against 48 KiB: 184
slots, 47,104 B at 32 lanes, just under. At 64 lanes it is 94,208 B, so a
wave64 build stops at that assert. Beside it, both tables are checked square,
`kb_block_dim` against `kWarpSize` and `kbr_block_dim`, and the two tables'
extents against `kExchangeMaxMomentum` and `kExchangeKbMaxMomentum`, the limits
`exchange_check`, the helpers and the build's storage read in place of the
tree-wide `kMaxAngularMomentum`. The footprint is `kernel_k`'s, unchanged.

**Scratch.** As J's is `CoulombPairClass`'s, K's is `OrderedPairClass`'s,
allocated with the records once per basis, so an SCF loop allocates none of it
per build. What depends only on the basis is written then too: a bra's `dc`
rows (`(l_max + 1) n_rec` `int32`s) and its block tables (6 `int32`s per
block). What depends on the density is rewritten by every call: `dcd` (one
`Dcd` per class), `geom` (4 doubles per group: the reach is screened at
`record_bound · G_max · max|D|`) and `factor` (a double per block, eq. 49 of
arXiv:2407.21445). So **one `exchange` or `exchange_block_factors` at a time
per set of classes**: two builds over the same classes must be queued on one
stream. At the end of a call every class's stream is forked from
`res.stream()`, which has joined them all, so a class's frees and the next
build's writes follow every other stream's reads of it.

What the call still allocates (one rank table per momentum; `3 n_ao` doubles
of AO centres and `n_ao` `int32`s of shell starts; the three doubles of the
decay and one of `G_max`) is drawn from `res.device()`'s pool in
`res.stream()`'s order and returned in its order once every phase is joined
back, so there is no host synchronization. The shell counts by momentum and
the basis's Cartesian AO count are read on the host off the classes
(`n_shells`, `n_ao`); `exchange` checks the density's order against the
latter. The records themselves are device only.

**One text serves both warp widths.** `kb` and `kbr` are emitted `any_wavefront`: its
shuffles and its shared array read `FJ_WAVEFRONT`, which `deps/CMakeLists.txt`
sets from `WWR_WARP_SIZE`.

**Generated kernels are committed, not built.** `tools/emit_exchange_kernels.py`
writes `kernels/kb/` and `kernels/kbr/` (with `kbr_tables.h`) from the
FelizJueves submodule; re-run it after bumping the
submodule. `kernels/.clang-format` turns formatting off, and
`devtools/check-int-types.py` skips a file by FelizJueves's banner, so the files
stay byte-for-byte as emitted: a fix belongs in the generator.

## Not done

- **The paper's looser threshold.** `τ* = τ/N*` for K (§4.3.1 reports
  `5e-6`, its benchmarks run `1e-5`) is not taken: `tau` is a guarantee here.
  A looser K threshold would be its own change, measured.
- **The decay pays only at length.** A group's reach is set by its most
  diffuse primitive pair, 12–23 bohr for hydrogen in cc-pVDZ at
  `tau = 1e-10` (longer at the default), so `F_ab` drops below `max|D|` only for shells more than
  about twice that apart; and an RHF density in cc-pVDZ falls only about three
  orders over 20 bohr. A smaller `tau` lengthens every reach: on the 23-bohr
  H₁₀ chain it is 1.3% of the blocks at the default `1e-13` (4% at `1e-10`, 7%
  at `1e-6`); on H₂O none. The gain is for molecules well past 50 bohr, and
  its wall-clock is unmeasured.
- **Primitive-pair pruning** (#132's second part, arXiv:2407.21445 §3.2):
  measured, and no gain, so the ordered classes still hold every record, a far
  pair's exactly-zero bound among them. With the groups sorted by the dressed
  bound `kb` stops at the first record under the cutoff, so a pruned record
  is never visited, and K's time is in its blocks, whose number pruning does
  not change (no group empties: each holds its shell against itself, whose
  pair factor is 1). The prototype cut
  each group after its last record with `I · max I · 2 >= tau`: under
  `|D| <= 2` (the cap J's pruning states, `../coulomb/README.md`, "Pruning")
  no skipped term of a pruned record could pass `kb`'s screen. On the
  RX 9060 XT, K at the default `1e-13`, median of 11 runs, over the H₂ chains
  and densities that section describes:

  | Chain | Ordered records kept | K, every record | K, pruned | Change |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | H₁₀ | 1,526 of 2,500 (61%) | 6.56 ms | 6.50 ms | −0.9% |
  | H₂₀ | 3,546 of 10,000 (35%) | 18.4 ms | 18.3 ms | −0.6% |
  | H₅₀ | 9,606 of 62,500 (15%) | 78.9 ms | 76.5 ms | −3.0% |
  | H₁₀₀ | 19,706 of 250,000 (7.9%) | 183 ms | 182 ms | −0.3% |
  | H₂₀₀ | 39,906 of 1,000,000 (4.0%) | 401 ms | 399 ms | −0.5% |

  Run to run, K varies by a few percent at H₁₀ and H₂₀, so none of these is a
  gain. K moved by at most 2e-16 · max|K|. What pruning would save is memory:
  the records grow quadratically with the chain, about 108 bytes each, 108 MB
  on the device at H₂₀₀ (and, when this was measured, as much again pinned;
  `mmd` has since stopped staging records on the host), against 4 MB pruned.
  J does prune (`coulomb_pair_classes`): its bras are screened on `mmd`'s
  undressed bound, so there pruning saves most of its time (91% at H₁₀₀).
- **The swap inside the diagonal blocks.** An `a == b` block is computed
  whole, so its quartets are still visited twice: `O(n_shells)` blocks of
  `O(n_shells^2)`.
- **Batching.** One `kb` launch per (bra class, ket class), every block at
  once, as FelizJueves ships it; no split class.
- **The rest of the reuse across SCF iterations.** The classes' scratch and
  tables are allocated once per basis, but the per-call buffers outside them
  are not: the rank tables, the AO centres, the shell starts and `G_max`
  depend only on the basis and are recomputed per call anyway, and the decay
  (four doubles) is per density.
- **`kb` is not measured against `kernel_k`.** Only the pruning above timed
  K at all; FelizJueves has not timed `kb` against `kernel_k` either (its
  `measure.sh kblock` run was refused by the card gate).

## Public API

| Symbol | Role |
|---|---|
| `exchange(res, classes, density, k, tau = 1e-13, symmetry = DensitySymmetry::Symmetric)` | `Status`: the Cartesian K of `density` into `k`, from the whole set of `ordered_pair_classes` of the same basis, in its order, screened at `tau` (`0` screens nothing), by the `a <= b` blocks, the rest mirrored as `symmetry` says (a contract on `density`, unchecked). Writes the classes' scratch. InvalidConfig for a negative or NaN `tau`, another device, a density of another order, a class above f, classes of a basis of another order, a subset or reordering of the classes, or `k.n()^2` past `INT32_MAX` |
| `DensitySymmetry` | `Symmetric` or `Antisymmetric`: the density's, which `K` shares, and the mirror's sign. A general `D` is one call per part |
| `exchange_block_factors(res, classes, density, tau = 1e-13)` | `Result<std::vector<double>>`: every block's density factor `F_ab` as `exchange` makes it for the same arguments (the same code path, with no `K`: no zeroing, no `kb`, no mirror), read back, bra class major, then ket class, then table order. A diagnostic: how many blocks the decay screens below `max\|D\|` decides whether it pays. Synchronizes `res.stream()` |
| `OrderedPairClass`, `ordered_pair_classes`, ... | re-exported from [`pairs/`](pairs/README.md) |

## Tests

`test/gtest/exchange_gpu.cpp`: `K` of H₂O in STO-3G and cc-pVDZ (their
Hilbert densities), of an H₁₀ chain in cc-pVDZ (five H₂ 4 bohr apart, its
converged RHF density, which decays), and of CO in cc-pVTZ (an f shell on each
atom, so every class through `(ff|ff)`, the 175 with an f shell on `kbr`:
`kbr`'s first libcint check on a molecule) against libcint's `vk` of the same
density, through the golden data in `test/gtest/data/exchange/`, within
`1e-12 · max|K|` at `tau = 0`, at the default `1e-13` and at `1e-14` (the table
above; each run records its worst error and how far the screen moved `K`).
The chain's block factors as the build makes them (`exchange_block_factors`):
none above `max|D|`, and 24 of 1,860 below it at `1e-13`, 72 at `1e-10`, 121
at `1e-6`, all pinned. `K` symmetric to `1e-12 · max|K|`. A **non-symmetric**
density (the recorded one plus an antisymmetric part) as two sweeps, its
symmetric part's (`Symmetric`) plus its antisymmetric part's
(`Antisymmetric`), at `tau = 0` and the default: the symmetric part's `K`
against libcint's `vk` (the symmetric part is the recorded density; libcint
gave no `K` of a non-symmetric one), and the sum against `K[D]` assembled from
computed blocks only, by `K[D]^T = K[D^T]` (the `a <= b` blocks of `D`'s sweep
and the transposes of `D^T`'s), so a wrong mirror sign would miss by
`2 |K[A]|`; the antisymmetric part's `K` antisymmetric to round-off. A `tau`
above every bound gives exactly zero, which shows the screen reaches the
kernel and fires; the zeroing; and the refusals, a subset or a reordering of
the classes among them. The helpers are `exchange_helpers_gpu.cpp`'s test,
each against a host loop: the block table and its factors (every branch of
the rule), the shell starts and the mirror of either sign, the bound maximum,
the AO centres and group geometry, and the
decay fit on H₂O's and the chain's densities, whose bound is checked at every
off-centre element with no tolerance (the chain's `Λ` 0.397 / bohr, `H_off`
0.571, `max|D|` 0.328; FelizJueves's reference gives the same). The E
tables are `exchange_e_table_gpu.cpp`'s: every record of every ordered shell
pair of H₂O/cc-pVDZ and CO/cc-pVTZ (every class through `(f, f)`), as a bra and

(`test/gtest/data/e_table/`), each entry within 16 eps of its record's largest

libcint counterpart. The classes,
their `group_start`, `dc` rows and block tables among them, are
