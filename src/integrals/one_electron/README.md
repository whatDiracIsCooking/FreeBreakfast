# One-electron

One-electron integral matrices on the GPU, over kernels FelizJueves generates:
the overlap S, the kinetic energy T, the nuclear attraction V, and the core
Hamiltonian H = T + V; the overlap between two bases, `overlap_mixed`; the
dipole matrices mu_x, mu_y, mu_z; and the nuclear-gradient and nuclear-Hessian
contractions of S, T and V with an AO matrix, and the derivative matrices S^x,
T^x and V^x, `gradient` and `hessian`; and the dipole's nuclear-gradient
contraction, `dipole_gradient`.

## Module

`free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron`, a convenience module that re-exports
the ten below (and is itself re-exported by `free_breakfast.integrals`):

```
launch/            free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.launch: the per-class launch and the shared pre-launch checks
overlap/           free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.overlap: the overlap S
overlap_mixed/     free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.overlap_mixed: S between two bases
kinetic/           free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.kinetic: the kinetic energy T
potential_en/      free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.potential_en: the electron-nuclear potential V
dipole/            free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.dipole: the dipole matrices mu_x, mu_y, mu_z, through d
dipole_gradient/   free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.dipole_gradient: Σ M dμ^c/dR per component and atom, through f
gradient/          free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.gradient: Σ M dX/dR per atom, and dX/dR as matrices, for X = S, T, V
hessian/           free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.hessian: Σ M d²X/dR dR, 3N x 3N, for X = S, T, V
core_hamiltonian/  free_breakfast.integrals.one_electron.core_hamiltonian: H = T + V, in place
```

## Design

`mmd`'s `primitive_pair_classes` puts a basis's records on the device once per
basis, and a `Molecule` its nuclei (Z and position) at construction, so once
per geometry. A build then zeroes a `CartesianMatrixAO` and launches one generated
kernel per class, one thread per record: `overlap<la, lb>` for S,
`kinetic<la, lb>` for T, `potential_en<la, lb>` for V (the nuclei serial inside
each record). Each thread adds
its values at `(m, n)` and `(n, m)` with fp64 atomics, so a matrix is symmetric
to rounding (the two mirror positions are summed in different orders), not
bitwise. The spherical matrix is `MatrixAOPair::cartesian_to_spherical` of the
result.

**One stream per class.** `primitive_pair_classes` gives every class its own
`DeviceStream` on `res.device()`, and its records are allocated and copied
there. A build zeroes the matrix on `res.stream()`, then `launch`'s
`launch_each` forks every class's stream from it, launches each class on its
own stream, and joins them all back, so the classes run concurrently and
whatever follows on `res.stream()` sees the whole matrix. The atomics are what
make that safe: classes adding into one matrix at once do not race. H is still
T then V: each build joins before the next forks.

**One layout per kernel family.** `overlap/`, `overlap_mixed/`, `kinetic/`,
`potential_en/`, `dipole/` and `dipole_gradient/` are each the same set of files, named after the
directory (`gradient/` and `hessian/` too, each with its three families sharing one `kernels/`):

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `<family>.cppm` / `<family>.cpp` | The module and its build |
| `<family>_bridge.h` | The launcher's declaration, over `mmd`'s and `molecule`'s device views, shared by the `.cu` and the implementation unit's global module fragment |
| `<family>_launch.cu` | The launcher, the family's own device library |
| `kernels/` | The generated kernels and their header `<family>.h`, and the `.clang-format` that leaves them unformatted |
| `CMakeLists.txt` | The device library `….<family>.device` (the launcher and every kernel) and the module |

Every family takes its inputs, and its pre-launch checks
(`check_one_electron_build`, `check_nuclei`), from `launch`, its kernel table
from its generated header. Each launcher is bounded by its own table's extent;
`check_one_electron_build` holds every class to `launch`'s `kOneElectronMaxL`
(f), the engine's own limit rather than `basis`'s tree-wide
`kMaxAngularMomentum` (f too, checked equal in `launch.cpp`). `core_hamiltonian/` has no
kernels of its own, so it is only the module.

**H is built in place.** `core_hamiltonian` checks every input, then
`kinetic` zeroes H and writes T, and `add_nuclear_attraction` adds V on top.
Both only add, so T + V is formed without a T or V matrix of its own and
without a separate addition.

**The kernels.** Each family's `kernels/{s,t,v}_{00,01,02,03,11,12,13,22,23,33}.cu` (every
`la <= lb <= 3`; the dipole's `mu_{00,01,02,11,12,22}.cu`, `la <= lb <= 2`), generated by `tools/emit/one_electron_kernels.py` and
committed as emitted. They are FelizJueves's vendor-neutral `gpu` spelling:
each includes FelizJueves's own `felizjueves/runtime.h` (`felizjueves::headers`,
linked PRIVATE by every family's device library), which selects the vendor
runtime from the compiler's own macros, so the same text compiles under nvcc
and clang `-x hip` with nothing of wwr in it. They have no warp shuffle, so no
wavefront is written into them either.

**One kernel template per family, and its table.** Each class is written as
one explicit specialization of the family's kernel template (`overlap<0, 1>`),
never as a C name per class. The generator also writes one header,
`kernels/<family>.h`, which every unit includes: the template's primary
(`template <uint8_t La, uint8_t Lb>`, defined nowhere), each class's
specialization declared, and `<family>_kernels`, the `[la][lb]` table, null
where `la > lb`. Each unit is a source of the family's device library,
compiled on its own; the launcher includes only the header,
takes its limit from the table's extent (`static_assert`ing it square), and
indexes it. No class is named by hand, and a class the table names with no
unit is a link error. Launching a specialization another TU defines wants
relocatable device code under nvcc (warning #20280 otherwise), which is the
device library's default; hipcc takes it either way.

**Each of V's kernels has its own Boys function.** Every `v_*.cu` carries the
same `static __device__ double fj_boys(int32_t, double)` (FelizJueves's wide
one: the Kummer series below T = 125, the asymptote above, because a point
charge has no exponent to damp `T = ζ|P − C|²`). `static` keeps the ten copies
from colliding at the device link.

**The dipole is one launch for three matrices, and stops at d.** Its kernel
writes x, y and z of one record from one set of E tables, into a single
`[3, n_ao, n_ao]` array, so `dipole` builds into a region of
`res.device_workspace()` and copies each component into its own
`CartesianMatrixAO`. The origin and the charge are the kernel's run-time
arguments; the launcher passes 0 and 1. FelizJueves validates the kernel
through (dd) only and its emitter refuses f, so `kDipoleMaxL` is 2 and a class
with an f shell is `InvalidConfig` before anything is launched.

**Limits.**

- f per shell (`kOneElectronMaxL`), d for the dipole (`kDipoleMaxL`); a class
  above is `InvalidConfig`, before anything is launched.
- The kernels index the matrix as a 32-bit `m * n_ao + n`, so
  `n_ao² ≤ INT32_MAX` (about 46k Cartesian AOs); above is `InvalidConfig`.
  The dipole's index runs over all three components, so `3 n_ao² ≤ INT32_MAX`
  (about 26k).
- The records' AO offsets are not checked against the matrix's order: the
  classes must come from the basis the matrix was made for.
- V is one thread per record with every nucleus serial inside it, and its
  kernels spill registers from `la + lb = 3` (FelizJueves's
  `docs/implementation/one_electron.md`). A warp per record is FelizJueves's
  named follow-up.

## Public API

Everything the ten sub-modules export; each README has its own table.

| Symbol | Module |
|---|---|
| `launch_each`, the checks | `launch` |
| `overlap(res, classes, s)` | `overlap` |
| `overlap_mixed(res, rows, cols, s)`, `overlap_mixed_spherical(…)` | `overlap_mixed` |
| `kinetic(res, classes, t)` | `kinetic` |
| `nuclear_attraction(res, classes, molecule, v)`, `add_nuclear_attraction(…)` | `potential_en` |
| `core_hamiltonian(res, classes, molecule, h)` | `core_hamiltonian` |
| `dipole(res, classes, mu_x, mu_y, mu_z)`, `kDipoleMaxL` | `dipole` |
| `dipole_gradient(res, basis, classes, m)` | `dipole_gradient` |
| `overlap_gradient(res, basis, classes, m)`, `kinetic_gradient(…)`, `potential_gradient(res, basis, classes, molecule, m)` | `gradient` |
| `overlap_derivative(res, basis, classes)`, `kinetic_derivative(…)`, `potential_derivative(res, basis, classes, molecule)` | `gradient` |
| `overlap_hessian(res, basis, classes, m)`, `kinetic_hessian(…)`, `potential_hessian(res, basis, classes, molecule, m)` | `hessian` |

Every build is queued on `res.stream()`, each class on its own stream forked
from it and joined back into it, with no host synchronization.

## Usage

```cpp
import free_breakfast.integrals;
import free_breakfast.recipes.matrix_ao;
namespace fb = free_breakfast;

const auto classes = fb::primitive_pair_classes(res, basis);  // once per basis
const fb::Molecule molecule(res, atoms);                       // nuclei: once per geometry
fb::CartesianMatrixAO s(res, basis);
fb::CartesianMatrixAO h(res, basis);
fb::Status status = fb::overlap(res, *classes, s);
status = fb::core_hamiltonian(res, *classes, molecule, h);
```

## Tests

- `test/gtest/overlap_gpu.cpp`: H₂O in STO-3G and cc-pVDZ against libcint's
  `int1e_ovlp` at 1e-12 · max|S|, plus the zeroing and the class-range refusal.
- `test/gtest/core_hamiltonian_gpu.cpp`: the same two molecules' T, V and H
  against libcint's `int1e_kin` and `int1e_nuc` and PySCF's `hcore`, at the
  same tolerance, plus each build's zeroing and class-range refusal (off,
  with the three modules).
- `test/gtest/dipole_gpu.cpp`: H₂O in STO-3G and cc-pVDZ, carried to the
  spherical layout, against libcint's `int1e_r_sph` (`test/gtest/data/cis/`)
  at 1e-12 absolute over PySCF's unit-normalised functions, plus the zeroing and the refusal of an f shell and of
  matrices of different orders.
- `test/gtest/dipole_gradient_gpu.cpp`: the dipole's gradient contraction of
  the same two waters against libcint's `int1e_irp`, recorded by PySCF
  (`test/gtest/data/dipole_derivative/`), at 1e-10 of max|G|, its sum rule,
  and the refusals; `dipole_gradient/README.md` has the detail.
- `test/gtest/one_electron_gradient_gpu.cpp`: the gradient contractions of
  H₂O in cc-pVDZ and cc-pVTZ against libcint's derivative integrals, recorded
  by PySCF (`test/gtest/data/gradient/`), at 1e-10 of max|G|, each one's sum
  over atoms, and the refusals; `gradient/README.md` has the detail.
- `test/gtest/one_electron_derivative_gpu.cpp`: the same waters' derivative
  matrices S^x, T^x and V^x element by element against libcint
  (`test/gtest/data/one_electron_derivative/`), and contracted against the
  gradient contractions; `gradient/README.md` has the detail.
- `test/gtest/one_electron_hessian_gpu.cpp`: the Hessian contractions of the
  same water against libcint's second-derivative integrals, assembled as
  PySCF's `partial_hess_elec` does (`test/gtest/data/hessian/`), at 1e-11 of
  max|H|, each one's translational sum rule (its diagonal blocks are formed by
  it, from the off-diagonal ones), `molecule`'s
  `nuclear_repulsion_hessian` against `hess_nuc`, and the refusals;
  `hessian/README.md` has the detail.
- `test/gtest/launch_gpu.cpp`: `check_nuclei` accepting nuclei on the call's
  device. The nuclei themselves are `molecule`'s, tested in
  `test/gtest/molecule_gpu.cpp`.
