Orbital-optimized NEVPT2

Design note for experimental/oo_nevpt2/. Status: an idea and one probe, nothing under src/. The probe says seven things: the bare functional collapses (Results); natural orbitals of the PT2-corrected density move the energy by only tenths of a millihartree (Natural orbitals); orbitals that minimize the weight of the first-order wave function, a bounded and parameter-free replacement, move the energy away from FCI (Maximum overlap); the exact state's own natural orbitals recover only 2 to 5% of NEVPT2's error on water (Against FCI); the energy minimized with that weight capped at its CASSCF value converges and moves 0.2 mEh towards FCI, but the cap's value decides the answer (Capping the weight); and on the case the idea was for, LiF's state-averaged ionic and covalent pair, the natural orbitals of the correlated density move both QD-NEVPT2 states by 4 to 5 mEh and their crossing by 0.05 Å (The state average); and on ethylene's N and V states, where the choice of orbitals does move PC-QD-NEVPT2 by tenths of an eV, the passes settle at a different fixed point from every start, 0.75 eV apart (Ethylene).

The idea, and the loop that does not work

CASSCF chooses its orbitals to minimize the CASSCF energy, which contains almost no dynamic correlation. NEVPT2 then adds that correlation on top of orbitals that never saw it. The idea is to let the NEVPT2 energy inform the orbitals, so some dynamic correlation is built in at the orbital level.

The loop as first stated was: NEVPT2 informs the SCF orbitals, those feed a CASSCF, that feeds NEVPT2, and so on. It has no effect. CASSCF optimizes the orbitals to its own stationary point, so the NEVPT2 information only changes its starting guess and the loop returns the CASSCF orbitals it started with.

The fix is to drop the CASSCF orbital condition and replace it, not to feed it.

The functional

E(κ) = E_CAS(κ, c*(κ)) + E_PT2(κ, c*(κ))

c*(κ)   the CASCI root at the orbitals C exp(κ):  (H₀(κ) − E_CAS) c* = 0
E_PT2   the PC-NEVPT2 correlation energy of that root, core and virtual
        orbitals canonical over the root's generalized Fock

OO-NEVPT2 is the stationary point of E in the orbital rotations κ. The CI is a CASCI at every set of orbitals, so the reference stays a proper CAS eigenfunction and Dyall's Hamiltonian stays well defined.

The non-redundant rotations are the same three classes CASSCF has: core–active, active–virtual and core–virtual.

  • Active–active rotations leave both terms unchanged (CASCI and PC-NEVPT2 are invariant). SC-NEVPT2 is not invariant to them, which is one reason to build this on PC.
  • Core–core and virtual–virtual rotations are fixed by the canonical condition, not optimized. The functional is defined on canonical orbitals, and the derivative through that condition is the zc term below.

The gradient is already in the tree

The NEVPT2 nuclear gradient (src/nevpt2/gradient/) makes this Lagrangian stationary at CASSCF orbitals:

L = E_CAS + E_PT2 + Z · g_orb + z · g_ci + ⟨z^c, f⟩

It needs the Z-vector (Z, z) because E_PT2 is not stationary in the orbitals or the CI vector. That non-stationarity is exactly the OO-NEVPT2 gradient. Piece by piece:

QuantityMeaning hereCall
Acorr = X − Xᵀ∂E_PT2/∂κ at fixed c nevpt2::pc_adjoint (a)

zc, Az^c, yc the derivative through the canonical condition nevpt2::zc_multipliers, nevpt2::zc_adjoint
g∂E_CAS/∂κ; zero at CASSCF orbitals, not afterwards response::fock (gradient)
H̃the CASSCF orbital Hessian with the CI relaxed response::Hessian, response::RelaxedHessian

With c eliminated through the CASCI condition, the total derivative is

dE/dκ = g + A − H_oc H_cc⁻¹ y          A = A^corr + A^{z^c},  y ← y + y^c

and the Z-vector equation the gradient code solves is

H̃ Z = −(g + A − H_oc H_cc⁻¹ y),        H̃ = H_oo − H_oc H_cc⁻¹ H_co

So Z is the quasi-Newton step of OO-NEVPT2: the total gradient, preconditioned by the CI-relaxed CASSCF Hessian, with the second derivatives of E_PT2 dropped. The existing right-hand side already carries g, so the same solve gives the step away from CASSCF convergence too. Each iteration is one NEVPT2 adjoint and one Z-vector solve, with no derivative integrals.

Algorithm

  1. Start from a converged single-state DF-CASSCF (C, c).
  2. pc_adjoint, zc_multipliers, zc_adjoint at (C, c): E_PT2, A, y.
  3. response::zvector with the right-hand sides g + pack(A) and ½ y: the step Z.
  4. C ← C exp(t κ(Z)), with t from a short line search on E.
  5. CASCI at the new C, then canonicalize the core and virtuals over the new root's Fock.
  6. Stop when ‖g + pack(A)‖ and the reduced gradient are below tolerance; otherwise go to 2.

A converged OO-NEVPT2 has no orbital Z-vector in its nuclear gradient. The CI response z remains, because c is stationary for E_CAS only.

Risks

  • No lower bound. E_PT2 is not variational in the orbitals. OO-MP2 overcorrelates by shrinking denominators and needs regularization. Dyall's Hamiltonian keeps NEVPT2 free of intruders at fixed orbitals; an optimizer may look for them. PC's conditioning floor (pc_floor) is the existing lever.
  • Active-space drift. Nothing ties the active orbitals to the static correlation. They may rotate towards whatever raises |E_PT2|.
  • Hessian quality. The CASSCF Hessian ignores E_PT2's curvature. If the two disagree, steps overshoot and convergence is slow.
  • Cost. Each iteration is a NEVPT2 energy, its reverse pass (the 3-RDM and contracted 4-RDM adjoints) and a Z-vector solve.
  • Several states. For a state average or QD-NEVPT2 the functional is not obvious (the average of the PT2 energies, or a trace of Heff). Out of scope here.
  • Prior art. Not checked. OO-MP2 and Brueckner methods are the single-reference analogues; a literature search for orbital-optimized CASPT2 or NEVPT2 comes before any real investment.

The experiment

experimental/oo_nevpt2/main.cpp, built by the experimental preset as free_breakfast_oo_nevpt2. It runs a config's DF-CASSCF through the pipeline, then iterates the algorithm above with PC-NEVPT2 and prints, per iteration:

  • E_CAS, E_PT2 and their sum;
  • ‖g‖, ‖pack(A)‖ and ‖g + pack(A)‖;
  • E at t = ½ and t = 1 along Z. On a quadratic model whose minimum is t = 1, the drop at ½ is ¾ of the drop at 1, so the ratio measures how well the CASSCF Hessian models E.
  • the active natural occupations.

The line search takes the lower of t = 1 and ½, doubles once to t = 2 while still descending, and halves down to 1/64 when neither descends. A Newton step is cut to 0.3 rad. Where the Z-vector solve refuses (the CASSCF Hessian no longer positive definite), the step is steepest descent on g + pack(A), 0.05 rad at its largest.

What decides. If the total energy moves by microhartrees and the orbitals barely change, the idea is not worth a module. If it moves by millihartrees, or shifts a crossing, it is, with regularization.

Results

verdict The bare functional has no usable minimum. In all three cases the total energy falls without the gradient shrinking, E_PT2 runs away while E_CAS rises, and the run stops only when PC-NEVPT2 refuses an intruder. This is the "no lower bound" risk above, and it is not a small effect: do not promote this as it stands.

What the probe does not show

  • Once the Hessian is indefinite the step is steepest descent on g + pack(A), without the CI's response, so the later rows are not the exact reduced gradient's path. The early rows are.
  • It does not prove there is no shallow local minimum that a trust-region method on E's own Hessian would find. Nothing in the first steps suggests one: the gradient does not shrink.
  • One basis and one small active space per molecule; SC-NEVPT2 not tried.

Why it collapses, and what else could be minimized

Orbital rotations form a compact set, so a functional continuous in the orbitals is bounded below. E_PT2 = −V†(H₀ − E₀)⁻¹V is not, because it has poles: rotating occupied orbitals into the external spaces breaks the Brillouin condition, the reference then couples to single-excitation-like perturbers in step with g, and the rotated Dyall Hamiltonian's denominators shrink. The Hylleraas form does not help: it is variational in the amplitudes, and its minimum over them is the same expression. (The mechanism is read off the numbers above, not isolated block by block.)

Natural orbitals: how much is there to gain?

Before building any of those, the prior question is whether orbitals that have seen dynamic correlation change the NEVPT2 energy by an amount that matters. The probe's no mode answers it without minimizing anything:

  1. D = D_CAS + ∂E_PT2/∂h, the unrelaxed response density: pc_adjoint's h_bar plus the canonical condition's zc. Its trace correction is zero to round-off.
  2. Diagonalize D over all orbitals. The new active orbitals are the natural orbitals with the most weight on the old active space, the new core those of the rest with the most on the old core (by overlap, so a correlated core orbital does not swap in by occupation).
  3. CASCI and PC-NEVPT2 in them; repeat.

answer Tenths of a millihartree. The first pass lowers the total by 0.06 to 0.5 mEh (0.04 to 0.3 kcal/mol); the difference between the two LiF geometries is 0.27 mEh. E_PT2 moves by up to 1.7 mEh, but E_CAS gives back 70% of it. On these cases the orbitals NEVPT2 would prefer are, for the energy, the CASSCF orbitals.

  • The spaces barely mix. The old active space keeps 3.9993 to 3.9998 of its 4 units of weight, and the largest density element between spaces is 2.6e-2.
  • Iterating is not a method. Water settles (−0.125 mEh, the mixing down to 4e-5). On LiF the total climbs back through zero over six passes while the mixing is still 3e-4 to 2e-3: nothing is being minimized, so nothing says the fixed point is lower.
  • Against the collapse. The quasi-Newton path gains 1 to 7 mEh in its first step and hartrees later. The natural orbitals gain a tenth of that first step. Most of what OO-NEVPT2 "finds" is the pole, not better orbitals.

Maximum overlap: minimizing the first-order weight

The collapse needs a functional that is bounded below and has no threshold to choose. A bound on E_CAS's rise, on ‖g‖ or on the distance from the CASSCF orbitals is neither: the bare functional has no interior minimum, so such a bound is active at the solution and its value decides the answer. Brueckner's criterion is the candidate with physical content: the orbitals whose reference has the largest overlap with the exact state. To first order that overlap is (1 + W)−½, with

W(κ) = ⟨Ψ⁽¹⁾|Ψ⁽¹⁾⟩ = Σ_t Σ_k ( y_tk / (λ_k + Δ_t) )²      at the orbitals C exp(κ), CI a CASCI there

W is the weight of PC-NEVPT2's first-order wave function, never negative, and near a pole it grows as 1/D² where the energy grows as 1/D. The mo mode minimizes it and reads E_CAS + E_PT2 at the minimum, without minimizing the energy.

How the probe gets W and its gradient

  • W from the energy. No call reports the amplitudes' norm. Adding w S C d Cᵀ S to the core Hamiltonian, d = −1 on the core, 0 on the active and +1 on the virtual orbitals, moves the canonical orbital energies and nothing else, so each perturber's denominator rises by w n, n its count of indices outside the active space (1 for Sr and Si, 4 for Sijrs, 3 for Sijr and Srsi, 2 for the rest). A class's energy then has the derivative n W_class at w = 0, taken as a central difference over w = ±1e-3.
  • Checked by shifting the virtual orbitals alone and the core alone: the four classes with both kinds of index give the same W either way to 5e-9.
  • The gradient is a central difference over 2e-3 rad in each non-redundant rotation, the CASCI re-solved at every point. Rotations whose derivative vanishes at the CASSCF orbitals (33 of water's 54, by symmetry) are left out and re-checked at the end: at most 1.5e-7.
  • The step is BFGS from the differences' own diagonal curvature, backtracked on W, to max |dW/dκ| below 2e-5.

Result

Capping the weight

The other use of W is as a bound rather than a target: minimize the energy, and do not let the first-order wave function grow.

min over κ of  E_CAS(κ) + E_PT2(κ)     subject to  W(κ) ≤ f · W(CASSCF orbitals)

This is bounded below, which the bare functional is not: each perturber gives −y²/D to the energy and y²/D² to W, so E_PT2 ≥ −W · max D, and the denominators are bounded by the spread of the orbital energies. With f = 1 it has no number to choose: the reference may not carry less weight in the first-order state than CASSCF's does. The cap mode solves it; its fourth argument is f.

How the probe solves it

  • Both gradients by differences, as in the mo mode: E and W over ±2e-3 rad in each rotation, the CASCI re-solved at every point. 21 of water's 54 rotations have a derivative; the rest stay below 4e-8 at the solution.
  • The step is the quadratic program with the one linearized constraint, over a BFGS inverse Hessian of the Lagrangian E + λW seeded from its diagonal curvature, backtracked on the merit E + μ max(0, W − cap).
  • Converged when max |dE/dκ + λ dW/dκ| is below 2e-5 and W is within 1e-7 of the cap. Every run below converged, at full steps.

Result

answer It is well posed, it converges, and it moves towards FCI; but the cap decides by how much. At f = 1 the total falls by 0.19 mEh at equilibrium and 0.16 mEh stretched, 2 to 3% of NEVPT2's error. Allowing 3% more weight buys 1 mEh, and 10% buys 2 to 3 mEh. The constraint is active at every solution, so the energy is a function of f and nothing inside the functional picks f.

The state average: LiF across the crossing

Everything above is one state. The case the idea was for is a state average, whose orbitals are a compromise: LiF's ionic and covalent ¹Σ⁺ states want a different F 2pσ, F⁻'s diffuse and F's compact. The probe's sa mode puts the natural-orbital pass of Natural orbitals there, the cheapest test there is, and asks one thing: does the crossing of the two PC-QD-NEVPT2 states move?

  • D = D_SA + ∂E[W]/∂h, E[W] = Σ_I w_I H_II the weighted trace of the effective Hamiltonian's correlation part: qd_pc_adjoint's h_bar at W = diag(w) plus the canonical condition's zc. At equal weights the trace does not depend on how the model space is rotated, so D is the average of the QD states' unrelaxed correlated densities.
  • Its natural orbitals, the spaces picked by overlap as before; a CASCI of both roots in them, the core and virtuals canonical over the state-averaged Fock, and qd_pc_energy's H_eff, symmetrized as the pipeline's is. Repeat.
  • The crossing is read where H_eff's two diagonal elements are equal, by linear interpolation on a 0.25 Å grid from 5.5 to 7.0 Å. The CASCI roots are 22 to 63 mEh apart there over the passes, so they are the covalent (root 0) and ionic (root 1) states to a good approximation, and the gap at the crossing is 2|H₀₁|.

lif_sa.toml: SA-CASSCF(2,2) of two singlets at equal weights over F 2pσ and Li 2s, aug-cc-pVDZ, all-electron. On a 0.5 Å grid the CASSCF states come closest at 4.5 Å (27 mEh); PC-QD-NEVPT2 at the CASSCF orbitals puts the crossing at 6.34 Å.

Result

answer The crossing does not move. One pass shifts it by 0.46 Å, the next shifts it back past where it started, and the passes settle at 6.28 Å: 0.05 Å from the CASSCF orbitals' 6.34 Å. Both states rise by 4 to 5 mEh on the way, ten times what a single state moved, but they rise together: the difference between them at the fixed point is about 0.6 mEh.

The first correction mixes the spaces by 5.3e-2, twice the single-state LiF's 2.6e-2, and the CASCI energies shift by +12 and −9 mEh: the correlated density wants an F 2pσ that suits F⁻ better, and the ionic reference gains what the covalent one loses.   • PC-QD-NEVPT2 takes it back. Of the 21 mEh the first pass moves the two references apart, the perturbation theory returns 27: the ionic state ends 5.8 mEh higher against the covalent one, not lower. The references' orbital error is first order in the energy, and the singles-like perturbers were already correcting it.
  • One pass is misleading. It moves the crossing inward by 0.46 Å. The passes then alternate with a shrinking swing (5.88, 6.46, 6.12, 6.39, …, 6.27, 6.29 Å) while the mixing falls to 2.8e-4, so this iteration, unlike single-state LiF's, has a fixed point: the orbitals whose averaged correlated density has no element between the spaces, a Brueckner-like condition on the state average.

The measurement is that the answer barely depends on the choice, not which choice is right.

At 3.0 Å RHF orbital 5 is a 2pπ, the two roots do not couple (H₀₁ = 0) and the config's active would need to name orbital 3; the scan starts above that.

Ethylene's V state: where the orbitals do matter

Water and LiF are cases where the CASSCF orbitals are slightly off. Ethylene's V state (¹B₁u, π → π*) is the textbook case where they are wrong in kind: without the σ electrons' dynamic response the π* of a (2,2) space comes out far too diffuse, and perturbation theory on those orbitals is known to miss the vertical excitation by tenths of an eV. If correlated-density orbitals help anywhere, it is here. The sa mode runs unchanged; the only new files are two configs.

  • ethylene_sa.toml: SA-CASSCF(2,2) of the two lowest singlets, N and V, over π and π*, aug-cc-pVDZ, all-electron. The active pair is AVAS over C 2p at a threshold of 0.95, which only the two π orbitals pass (0.9 lets two more orbitals in). The two states differ in symmetry, so H₀₁ is zero to round-off and the gap between H_eff's diagonal elements is the vertical excitation energy.
  • The SA-CASSCF has two stationary points. The solver first reaches one with the average at −77.895776 (‖g‖ 1.5e-8 after five macro iterations), then leaves it for the minimum at −77.921394, 25.6 mEh lower. The first is a saddle. Between them the V reference moves by 57 mEh and N by 6: the CASCI gap is 7.84 eV at the saddle and 6.12 eV at the minimum. Read with the literature, the saddle is the valence-like π* and the minimum the diffuse one; the tree has no second-moment integrals, so the probe does not confirm that with ⟨x²⟩. ethylene_sa_valence.toml is the same config with the CASSCF tolerances at 1e-7 and 1e-6, which stopped at the saddle in three runs of three. At the tight tolerances one run of eight stopped there too.
  • The weights. The passes use the config's weights for both the CASSCF and the averaged density, so 0.25/0.75 and 0.75/0.25 (N/V) are two more starts: the config with a weights line added.

Result

answer The orbitals matter here, and the passes do not settle the matter. At the CASSCF orbitals the four starts give PC-QD-NEVPT2 excitation energies from 7.45 to 8.09 eV, a spread of 0.64 eV. Every start converges to a fixed point of the passes, and the four fixed points run from 7.44 to 8.18 eV: a spread of 0.75 eV. The passes move one start by 0.36 eV and the others by 0.1 eV or less, and never from one basin to the other.

  • This note's earlier claim was wrong. It said nothing suggested an untested case would differ by an order of magnitude. Here the choice of orbitals moves the PT2 excitation energy by 0.64 eV (23 mEh), against 0.6 mEh between LiF's states. The singles-like perturbers repair a reference that is slightly off; they do not repair a π* of the wrong size.
  • One start moves by 0.36 eV. With N at three quarters of the weight the CASSCF π* suits N, the V reference sits 9.4 eV up, and the passes rotate it: V's CASCI energy falls by 28 mEh, N's rises by 13, and PC-QD-NEVPT2's V rises by 15 mEh. The gap goes from 7.54 to 7.89 eV, towards the accepted value near 8 eV (theoretical best estimates of 7.9 to 8.0 eV, quoted and not recomputed here). By the bar set before the run (0.1 eV towards it, and the passes settle) this start passes.
  • The others do not. From the equal-weight minimum, the solution a converged SA-CASSCF returns and the one read as the diffuse π*, the gap stays at 7.44 eV, half an eV low: the first mixing is 1.5e-2, as large as the saddle's, and the gap changes by 0.014 eV. From the saddle the gap rises from 8.09 to 8.18 eV, away from the accepted value.
  • The fixed point is not unique. At equal weights the passes have at least two, 0.75 eV apart, each next to the CASSCF stationary point it started from. A pass is a local step, and what is wrong with the minimum's orbitals is not local. Across the weights the fixed points differ as well, but that much is by construction: the weights are in the averaged density.
  • The spread does not shrink. A criterion that picked the right orbitals would bring different starts together. The four gaps are 0.64 eV apart before the passes and 0.75 eV apart after.
  • An energy criterion would pick the wrong basin. At the two equal-weight fixed points N's energy agrees to 0.13 mEh, and V's is 27.6 mEh lower in the minimum's basin. Neither is variational, but a functional that minimized the PT2 energies, bounded or not, would be pulled to that side, the one half an eV from the accepted value.
  • Limits. One basis, the smallest active space, PC only, an unrelaxed density. No ⟨x²⟩, so "valence" and "diffuse" are read from the energies and the literature. The judge is a quoted number near 8 eV, not an FCI in this basis. The 0.75/0.25 start was not checked for a lower SA-CASSCF solution.

Where this leaves the idea

  • Not worth a module on this evidence. By the note's own bar (millihartrees, or a shifted crossing) the natural-orbital gain is too small, and a bounded functional would be a new energy and adjoint to recover a fraction of a millihartree. The FCI comparison sizes it for water: 0.14 to 0.48 mEh from the exact state's natural orbitals, and 0.16 to 0.19 mEh from the capped functional at f = 1.
  • Constraining the functional makes it well posed, not useful. The minimum of W moves the energy the wrong way, and W is not a measure of orbital quality. E under a cap on W converges and moves the right way, but its answer is set by the cap: with the one parameter-free choice it gains 2 to 3% of the error, and any larger gain is bought by choosing f. What remains untried is a functional with the Brillouin-violating part of V projected out, the analogue of OO-MP2's missing singles.
  • The case the idea was for says the same. A state average's orbitals are a compromise, and on LiF's ionic/covalent pair the correlated density does ask for different ones (The state average). QD-NEVPT2 was already correcting for it: at the natural-orbital fixed point the crossing has moved 0.05 Å and the two states 0.6 mEh against each other. The bar was a shifted crossing, and this is not one.
  • Ethylene shows the problem is real and this is not its answer. On the V state the orbitals move PC-QD-NEVPT2 by 0.64 eV (Ethylene), so there is something for an orbital criterion to fix. The natural-orbital passes are not that criterion: they have a fixed point in each CASSCF basin, they do not bring the starts together, and the PT2 energy itself is lower in the basin that is half an eV off. The usual remedy is a reference that contains the missing σ response (a larger or restricted active space), and nothing here competes with it.
  • What is still not tested: a judge for the state average (an FCI-sized pair), a relaxed density, 3d metals, and a single-set optimum as opposed to a natural-orbital fixed point.
  • If it is reopened, it is for the singles-projected functional on ethylene, with second moments to say which π* it finds, not for these orbitals.
