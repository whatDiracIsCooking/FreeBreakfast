Orbital-optimized NEVPT2

Design note for experimental/oo_nevpt2/. Status: an idea and one probe, nothing under src/. The probe says four things: the bare functional collapses (Results); natural orbitals of the PT2-corrected density move the energy by only tenths of a millihartree (Natural orbitals); orbitals that minimize the weight of the first-order wave function, a bounded and parameter-free replacement, move the energy away from FCI (Maximum overlap); and the exact state's own natural orbitals recover only 2 to 5% of NEVPT2's error on water (Against FCI).

The idea, and the loop that does not work

CASSCF chooses its orbitals to minimize the CASSCF energy, which contains almost no dynamic correlation. NEVPT2 then adds that correlation on top of orbitals that never saw it. The idea is to let the NEVPT2 energy inform the orbitals, so some dynamic correlation is built in at the orbital level.

The loop as first stated was: NEVPT2 informs the SCF orbitals, those feed a CASSCF, that feeds NEVPT2, and so on. It has no effect. CASSCF optimizes the orbitals to its own stationary point, so the NEVPT2 information only changes its starting guess and the loop returns the CASSCF orbitals it started with.

The fix is to drop the CASSCF orbital condition and replace it, not to feed it.

The functional

E(κ) = E_CAS(κ, c*(κ)) + E_PT2(κ, c*(κ))

c*(κ)   the CASCI root at the orbitals C exp(κ):  (H₀(κ) − E_CAS) c* = 0
E_PT2   the PC-NEVPT2 correlation energy of that root, core and virtual
        orbitals canonical over the root's generalized Fock

OO-NEVPT2 is the stationary point of E in the orbital rotations κ. The CI is a CASCI at every set of orbitals, so the reference stays a proper CAS eigenfunction and Dyall's Hamiltonian stays well defined.

The non-redundant rotations are the same three classes CASSCF has: core–active, active–virtual and core–virtual.

  • Active–active rotations leave both terms unchanged (CASCI and PC-NEVPT2 are invariant). SC-NEVPT2 is not invariant to them, which is one reason to build this on PC.
  • Core–core and virtual–virtual rotations are fixed by the canonical condition, not optimized. The functional is defined on canonical orbitals, and the derivative through that condition is the zc term below.

The gradient is already in the tree

The NEVPT2 nuclear gradient (src/nevpt2/gradient/) makes this Lagrangian stationary at CASSCF orbitals:

L = E_CAS + E_PT2 + Z · g_orb + z · g_ci + ⟨z^c, f⟩

It needs the Z-vector (Z, z) because E_PT2 is not stationary in the orbitals or the CI vector. That non-stationarity is exactly the OO-NEVPT2 gradient. Piece by piece:

Acorr = X − Xᵀ∂E_PT2/∂κ at fixed c nevpt2::pc_adjoint (a)

With c eliminated through the CASCI condition, the total derivative is

dE/dκ = g + A − H_oc H_cc⁻¹ y          A = A^corr + A^{z^c},  y ← y + y^c

and the Z-vector equation the gradient code solves is

H̃ Z = −(g + A − H_oc H_cc⁻¹ y),        H̃ = H_oo − H_oc H_cc⁻¹ H_co

So Z is the quasi-Newton step of OO-NEVPT2: the total gradient, preconditioned by the CI-relaxed CASSCF Hessian, with the second derivatives of E_PT2 dropped. The existing right-hand side already carries g, so the same solve gives the step away from CASSCF convergence too. Each iteration is one NEVPT2 adjoint and one Z-vector solve, with no derivative integrals.

Algorithm

  1. Start from a converged single-state DF-CASSCF (C, c).
  2. pc_adjoint, zc_multipliers, zc_adjoint at (C, c): E_PT2, A, y.
  3. response::zvector with the right-hand sides g + pack(A) and ½ y: the step Z.
  4. C ← C exp(t κ(Z)), with t from a short line search on E.
  5. CASCI at the new C, then canonicalize the core and virtuals over the new root's Fock.
  6. Stop when ‖g + pack(A)‖ and the reduced gradient are below tolerance; otherwise go to 2.

A converged OO-NEVPT2 has no orbital Z-vector in its nuclear gradient. The CI response z remains, because c is stationary for E_CAS only.

Risks

  • No lower bound. E_PT2 is not variational in the orbitals. OO-MP2 overcorrelates by shrinking denominators and needs regularization. Dyall's Hamiltonian keeps NEVPT2 free of intruders at fixed orbitals; an optimizer may look for them. PC's conditioning floor (pc_floor) is the existing lever.
  • Active-space drift. Nothing ties the active orbitals to the static correlation. They may rotate towards whatever raises |E_PT2|.
  • Hessian quality. The CASSCF Hessian ignores E_PT2's curvature. If the two disagree, steps overshoot and convergence is slow.
  • Cost. Each iteration is a NEVPT2 energy, its reverse pass (the 3-RDM and contracted 4-RDM adjoints) and a Z-vector solve.
  • Several states. For a state average or QD-NEVPT2 the functional is not obvious (the average of the PT2 energies, or a trace of Heff). Out of scope here.
  • Prior art. Not checked. OO-MP2 and Brueckner methods are the single-reference analogues; a literature search for orbital-optimized CASPT2 or NEVPT2 comes before any real investment.

The experiment

experimental/oo_nevpt2/main.cpp, built by the experimental preset as free_breakfast_oo_nevpt2. It runs a config's DF-CASSCF through the pipeline, then iterates the algorithm above with PC-NEVPT2 and prints, per iteration:

  • E_CAS, E_PT2 and their sum;
  • ‖g‖, ‖pack(A)‖ and ‖g + pack(A)‖;
  • E at t = ½ and t = 1 along Z. On a quadratic model whose minimum is t = 1, the drop at ½ is ¾ of the drop at 1, so the ratio measures how well the CASSCF Hessian models E.
  • the active natural occupations.

The line search takes the lower of t = 1 and ½, doubles once to t = 2 while still descending, and halves down to 1/64 when neither descends. A Newton step is cut to 0.3 rad. Where the Z-vector solve refuses (the CASSCF Hessian no longer positive definite), the step is steepest descent on g + pack(A), 0.05 rad at its largest.

What decides. If the total energy moves by microhartrees and the orbitals barely change, the idea is not worth a module. If it moves by millihartrees, or shifts a crossing, it is, with regularization.

Results

verdict The bare functional has no usable minimum. In all three cases the total energy falls without the gradient shrinking, E_PT2 runs away while E_CAS rises, and the run stops only when PC-NEVPT2 refuses an intruder. This is the "no lower bound" risk above, and it is not a small effect: do not promote this as it stands.

What the probe does not show

  • Once the Hessian is indefinite the step is steepest descent on g + pack(A), without the CI's response, so the later rows are not the exact reduced gradient's path. The early rows are.
  • It does not prove there is no shallow local minimum that a trust-region method on E's own Hessian would find. Nothing in the first steps suggests one: the gradient does not shrink.
  • One basis and one small active space per molecule; SC-NEVPT2 not tried.

Why it collapses, and what else could be minimized

Orbital rotations form a compact set, so a functional continuous in the orbitals is bounded below. E_PT2 = −V†(H₀ − E₀)⁻¹V is not, because it has poles: rotating occupied orbitals into the external spaces breaks the Brillouin condition, the reference then couples to single-excitation-like perturbers in step with g, and the rotated Dyall Hamiltonian's denominators shrink. The Hylleraas form does not help: it is variational in the amplitudes, and its minimum over them is the same expression. (The mechanism is read off the numbers above, not isolated block by block.)

A true expectation value ⟨Ψ|H|Ψ⟩/⟨Ψ|Ψ⟩ over the reference plus the first-order wavefunction: an upper bound to FCI ⟨ψ₁|H|ψ₁⟩, towards the 5-RDM; not size-consistent

Natural orbitals: how much is there to gain?

Before building any of those, the prior question is whether orbitals that have seen dynamic correlation change the NEVPT2 energy by an amount that matters. The probe's no mode answers it without minimizing anything:

  1. D = D_CAS + ∂E_PT2/∂h, the unrelaxed response density: pc_adjoint's h_bar plus the canonical condition's zc. Its trace correction is zero to round-off.
  2. Diagonalize D over all orbitals. The new active orbitals are the natural orbitals with the most weight on the old active space, the new core those of the rest with the most on the old core (by overlap, so a correlated core orbital does not swap in by occupation).
  3. CASCI and PC-NEVPT2 in them; repeat.

answer Tenths of a millihartree. The first pass lowers the total by 0.06 to 0.5 mEh (0.04 to 0.3 kcal/mol); the difference between the two LiF geometries is 0.27 mEh. E_PT2 moves by up to 1.7 mEh, but E_CAS gives back 70% of it. On these cases the orbitals NEVPT2 would prefer are, for the energy, the CASSCF orbitals.

  • The spaces barely mix. The old active space keeps 3.9993 to 3.9998 of its 4 units of weight, and the largest density element between spaces is 2.6e-2.
  • Iterating is not a method. Water settles (−0.125 mEh, the mixing down to 4e-5). On LiF the total climbs back through zero over six passes while the mixing is still 3e-4 to 2e-3: nothing is being minimized, so nothing says the fixed point is lower.
  • Against the collapse. The quasi-Newton path gains 1 to 7 mEh in its first step and hartrees later. The natural orbitals gain a tenth of that first step. Most of what OO-NEVPT2 "finds" is the pole, not better orbitals.

Maximum overlap: minimizing the first-order weight

The collapse needs a functional that is bounded below and has no threshold to choose. A bound on E_CAS's rise, on ‖g‖ or on the distance from the CASSCF orbitals is neither: the bare functional has no interior minimum, so such a bound is active at the solution and its value decides the answer. Brueckner's criterion is the candidate with physical content: the orbitals whose reference has the largest overlap with the exact state. To first order that overlap is (1 + W)−½, with

W(κ) = ⟨Ψ⁽¹⁾|Ψ⁽¹⁾⟩ = Σ_t Σ_k ( y_tk / (λ_k + Δ_t) )²      at the orbitals C exp(κ), CI a CASCI there

W is the weight of PC-NEVPT2's first-order wave function, never negative, and near a pole it grows as 1/D² where the energy grows as 1/D. The mo mode minimizes it and reads E_CAS + E_PT2 at the minimum, without minimizing the energy.

How the probe gets W and its gradient

  • W from the energy. No call reports the amplitudes' norm. Adding w S C d Cᵀ S to the core Hamiltonian, d = −1 on the core, 0 on the active and +1 on the virtual orbitals, moves the canonical orbital energies and nothing else, so each perturber's denominator rises by w n, n its count of indices outside the active space (1 for Sr and Si, 4 for Sijrs, 3 for Sijr and Srsi, 2 for the rest). A class's energy then has the derivative n W_class at w = 0, taken as a central difference over w = ±1e-3.
  • Checked by shifting the virtual orbitals alone and the core alone: the four classes with both kinds of index give the same W either way to 5e-9.
  • The gradient is a central difference over 2e-3 rad in each non-redundant rotation, the CASCI re-solved at every point. Rotations whose derivative vanishes at the CASSCF orbitals (33 of water's 54, by symmetry) are left out and re-checked at the end: at most 1.5e-7.
  • The step is BFGS from the differences' own diagonal curvature, backtracked on W, to max |dW/dκ| below 2e-5.

answer It is well posed and it is wrong. W has a minimum and the minimization converges, but at both water geometries the orbitals it finds raise E_CAS + E_PT2, away from FCI: by 0.7 mEh where the active space is strongly correlated, and by 131 mEh where it is not.

  • W does not see the reference's energy. It is small where Dyall's denominators are large and the couplings small, whatever E_CAS is. At equilibrium E_CAS rises by 124 mEh while W says the reference improved, and |E_PT2| shrinks instead of recovering the loss. The first-order estimate of the overlap is not the overlap.

Against FCI: how much can orbitals buy?

None of these functionals is variational, so "better orbitals" needs an outside judge. Water in 6-31G is small enough for [fci] over all 13 orbitals and 10 electrons (1287² determinants) with the same density-fitted integrals (water_fci.toml, water_stretched_fci.toml). The probe prints the FCI energy and one more row: the functional in the FCI state's natural orbitals, the three most occupied as the core and the next four as the active space.

E_PT2 gains 1.80 and 0.75 mEh there and E_CAS gives back 1.66 and 0.27. What is left is second-order perturbation theory's own error, which no choice of orbitals in a (4,4) space removes.
  • The bare functional crosses the exact energy without slowing. At equilibrium it is 0.16 mEh above FCI after three Newton steps and 2.8 mEh below after four, with ‖g + A‖ still at 0.09. Any energy it shows on the way is an accident of where it was stopped.
  • The PT2 natural orbitals move the right way at equilibrium and the wrong way stretched, by 0.02 to 0.05 mEh either way: noise against a 6 to 9 mEh error.
  • Limits. One molecule, one small basis, one active space, single state. FCI natural orbitals picked by occupation are the usual choice, not a proven optimum for NEVPT2.

Where this leaves the idea

  • Not worth a module on this evidence. By the note's own bar (millihartrees, or a shifted crossing) the natural-orbital gain is too small, and a bounded functional would be a new energy and adjoint to recover a fraction of a millihartree. The FCI comparison puts a ceiling on it for water: 0.14 to 0.48 mEh, from orbitals no method here could beat.
  • Constraining the functional did not rescue it. The one parameter-free, bounded candidate (the minimum of W) moves the energy the wrong way, and W is not a measure of orbital quality. What remains untried is a functional with the Brillouin-violating part of V projected out, the analogue of OO-MP2's missing singles; the ceiling above says what it could gain here.
  • What was not tested is the case the idea was for: a reference whose orbitals CASSCF gets qualitatively wrong. Single-state ground-state LiF is not one; the ionic/covalent pair in a state average is, and so are ethylene's V state and 3d metals. Those need the multi-state functional, which this probe does not have.
  • If it is reopened, the order is: the natural-orbital pass on a state-averaged LiF pair across the crossing (through qd_pc_adjoint's h_bar); only if that moves the crossing, a Brillouin–Wigner or regularized functional on this probe.
