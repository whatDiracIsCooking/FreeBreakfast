rust/v0.0.4The card's stream under its lock, and the df-rhf chain

A design, not yet built. This page says what the third prototype of the redesign in docs/rust-redesign.html ("Rust rebuild: steps, the memory budget and rustflow") will build, and how it will be held. There is no crate beside it yet. Written 2026-11-25, after ../v0.0.3.

It covers two things:

  1. One handle set per card, revised. v0.0.3 could not build it as the design words it. Decided 2026-11-25: the card owns a stream, and the card's lock guards that stream with the handles. A step owns its own streams; a step that calls the library also borrows the card's.
  2. The DF-RHF chain as steps (item 3 of the design's order of work), on num_gpus cards, batched, over v0.0.3's core: the one-electron steps, the orthogonalizer, the guess, eri, and an SCF that calls build_jk on every trip.

Like its two predecessors it will be throwaway: a crate and a workspace of its own, in no gate, never merged into rust/.

Three marks are used below. Decided was agreed in discussion on 2026-11-25. Carried is v0.0.3's or the design's, unchanged. Proposed is this page's suggestion and not agreed.

What v0.0.3 could not build

The design has one BLAS handle per card, rebound to the holder's stream at every acquisition of the card's lock (SetStream, then SetWorkspace). v0.0.3 found three things in the way (its README, "What the prototype says to change", item 1):

  • fb_core_wwr::blas::BlasHandle keeps the Arc<Stream> it was made on and has no rebind.
  • fb_recipes_sys::trsm launches its diagonal kernels on that stream (blas.owner()) and its gemms through the handle. A handle rebound through the raw call would run one solve on two streams with nothing ordering them.
  • BlasHandle::bind_workspace takes ownership of the workspace, where the design's workspace is the card's and outlives every binding.

So v0.0.3 made a BLAS handle per library-using step per card: the form the design had rejected for its cost on HIP (58 MiB a handle).

The card's library: a stream, the handles and the workspace, under one lock

Decided. It replaces the rebind in the design's "One handle set per card, under one lock".

  • The card owns a stream. Each card's CardResources holds one stream, a BLAS handle and a dense-solver handle made on that stream, and the fixed 128 MiB BLAS workspace. The handles keep that stream for the life of the process. Nothing is rebound: SetStream is never called after creation, and the workspace is set once.
  • The lock guards all of it. CardResources is a Mutex around the stream, the handles and the workspace, not a Mutex<()> beside them. They are reachable only through the guard.
  • A step owns its streams. A GPU step has streams of its own on each card it spans: one by default, more if it declares them. The wrapper makes them in budget mode and keeps them in the step's slot.
  • A library-using step also borrows the card's. It declares the library per card in mem_budget, as in v0.0.3. For its run it holds that card's guard, and its library calls go on the card's stream.
  • The wrapper syncs all of them before release. Before run it takes the guard of every card the step declared, in card order. After run it synchronizes every stream the step has on each card, and each held card's stream, and only then drops the guards.
A step's own streams The card's stream
How many One per GPU step per card by default; more if declared One per card
Made By the wrapper, in budget mode In setup, with the handles
Reached through The step's lane The card's guard
What goes on it The step's kernels and copies Library calls; and a holder's kernels and copies, if it wants one order with no event
Who may use it Its step The step holding the card's lock, for its whole run

A step that calls no library holds no guard and overlaps other steps on the card as before.

Why this is not the old library stream. The design dropped the device's library stream on 2026-11-24 for a hole: the orthogonalizer enqueued syevd(S) on it, its wrapper synchronized the step's own, empty stream, and the SCF then wrote over syevd's work. Two things made the hole, and neither is here:

The library stream, as dropped The card's stream
Who may enqueue on it Any step, under a mutex held per call The lock's holder alone, for its whole run
What the wrapper synchronizes The step's own stream The step's own streams and the card's, before the lock is released

So a finished node is still finished device work, on every stream the step touched, and the scratch argument of the design is unchanged.

What it buys.

  • Safe by construction. The rebind was safe by measurement, on one release of each library: cuBLAS drops the workspace at SetStream, hipBLAS keeps it, and what the solver handles do was not measured. With no rebind there is nothing to measure and no sentinel test to keep.
  • Exclusive use by type. The handles are behind the guard, so "no handle is shared with work in flight" is the borrow checker's.
  • The recipes are right as written. trsm's kernels and its gemms land on one stream because the handle has only ever had one.
  • The default cap sees the handles. They are made in setup, so "one read per device, after its handles exist" holds as the design words it, and their memory no longer grows with the number of library-using steps.

What it gives up. Narrowing the lock to a step's library phases later. A holder's library calls are on the card's stream, so two library-using steps cannot interleave on a card without a second handle set. The design already holds the lock through a step's whole run and declined the ring of handle sets, so nothing decided is lost.

Not taken.

  • (a) A rebind. BlasHandle::rebind(&mut self, Arc<Stream>, workspace), with the recipes reading the stream from the current binding. It keeps "every step's library calls on its own stream", at the price of resting on the measured SetStream behaviour above.
  • (b) Every recipe takes its stream explicitly. It does not remove the rebind; it only moves the stream out of the handle.
  • Rebinding inside a step, per call, to each of its streams. Two library calls in flight on two streams would share the one fixed workspace.

A step with streams of its own and library calls

experimental/rust/df_batched/multi_gpu_spherical is the case: each card builds its share of B over a ring of two batches in flight, and every trip has kernels, a library solve and a download. v0.0.4's eri is built that way, so the rule above is run in the form that needs events.

Per card, with a ring of depth 2:

  • The ring's slots are the step's own streams, one per slot. A slot's stream carries its batch's table upload, its three-centre kernels and its download.
  • The fit goes on the card's stream. It waits on the event the slot recorded after its kernels, and the slot's download waits on the event recorded after the fit.
  • The host waits on a slot's last event, not on a whole stream, before it scatters that batch into B and writes the slot's pinned bytes again.
  • The metric's factor is on the card's stream before the loop, and overlaps the first batch's kernels.

The events are those the design keeps ForkStream for: a step's own inner fan-out. Whoever fans out, joins: every event is waited on before run returns, and the wrapper's sync is the last word.

What serializes. The two slots' fits run one after the other on the one card's stream. The ring overlaps a batch's copies and kernels with the previous batch's fit; it does not overlap two fits. Expected to cost little, since two solves on one card time-slice. Not measured.

Further handles are not built. A step that needs concurrent library calls inside itself declares handles of its own, each with a workspace it budgets, as the design already says. No step here needs it.

The ring is part of the layout. A slot's device and pinned bytes are tenants of eri's scratch, so the layout holds two of each, and the width is the widest whose ring fits the step's limit, as that experiment sizes it.

A simple library user needs none of this. The orthogonalizer and the SCF put everything on the card's stream, in the order they enqueue, with no event.

Where the handles and the workspace are made

  • Setup makes each card's stream and its two handles. Decided.
  • Setup then reads the card's free memory for the default cap. Carried.
  • The final budget node allocates the 128 MiB workspace and binds it to the card's BLAS handle, once. It stays a row of the budget table, charged against the cap. Carried. Binding it in setup would put it before the reading, and the row would then count it twice.
  • A device-free --budget-only makes no handles and reports each solver call's work buffer as zero. Carried.

The DF-RHF chain

v0.0.2's graph, over v0.0.3's core. What changes against v0.0.2: eri is v0.0.3's batched build with the ring above, there is no manager, the SCF calls build_jk itself, and every step runs on num_gpus cards or on gpu0.

Step Runs on Library Its Output Its scratch
harmonics gpu0 no U, the Cartesian-to-spherical transform The pinned staging

guess gpu0 no The SAD density The pinned staging
overlap gpu0 yes S The Cartesian matrix
core_hamiltonian gpu0 yes H = T + V The Cartesian matrix, the nuclei
orthogonalizer gpu0 yes X = S^(-1/2) syevd's work at n_ao, the eigenvectors

scf all GPUs yes, every card C, the orbital energies, D, the energy build_jk's per card; on gpu0 also F, the DIIS ring and syevd's work
  • The SCF's loop is inside its node. A condition loop in ScfStep's subflow, as the design has it; v0.0.2 had it in the outer graph. One trip is build_jk over the carried D, F, the energy, the residual, the DIIS step and the Roothaan step. Carried.
  • build_jk is v0.0.3's, unchanged: batches of aux functions, each card contracting its share, reduced onto gpu0 through the host, a blocking sync per batch. The SCF declares JkLayout's bytes per card in its own layout and passes its lease. Carried.
  • The SCF's solver calls are on gpu0's guard. syevd's work buffer is a tenant of the SCF's scratch, sized by the query in mem_budget, and the binding checks the slice against the query before the call. Carried.
  • Five steps hold gpu0's lock, and the DAG leaves several pairs of them unordered: overlap, core_hamiltonian and eri among themselves, and the orthogonalizer against the last two. This is the first DAG in which a step waits for a card's lock.
  • Crudest forms stay: K from D with no occupied factor, no screening, a neutral closed-shell molecule, built-in bases.

One measurement beside them, with no bar: each molecule's budget total against the old path's planner block for the same run (item 4 of the design's order of work).

One-off times are printed as v0.0.3's were: benzene's eri at 206 forced batches took 347 ms there with a blocking sync per batch, and the ring is what should move it. Not a benchmark; the real question is at gly10's size, which this prototype does not run.

Carried from v0.0.3, and what stays open

v0.0.3's README lists fourteen things for the design to change. Item 1 is settled above, and item 2 with it. The rest:

Proposed, about rust/. The library bindings and the recipes take the card's guard, stream and handles together, in place of a handle and a stream passed apart. A step with streams of its own can then not hand a recipe the wrong one, and blas.owner() goes. v0.0.4 wraps the calls it makes that way inside the prototype; the change to fb-core-wwr and fb-recipes-sys follows if it holds.

Not in it

  • Real multi-card. Two logical cards on device 0, as v0.0.3. A launch on the wrong card's context still cannot fail.
  • HIP. CUDA only. With no rebind, what was untested there is smaller: the workspace set once on a hipBLAS handle, and rocBLAS's hard need for it.
  • A step with further handles of its own, and two fits in flight on one card.
  • A ring in build_jk, and batches of B kept on the cards between passes: every SCF trip uploads all of B, a batch at a time.
  • gly10, or any size where B does not fit a card.
  • The enforcement checks: the clippy bans, the allocator record, the unbudgeted-heap row.
  • Screening, the default limit's value, packing Outputs, several nodes.
  • The replay reset. v0.0.2's reset mode is kept; what carries over is the SCF's orbitals and nothing in scratch.
