rust/v0.0.4The design, and the prototype built from scratch

The design of the Rust rebuild as it stands at v0.0.4, and what v0.0.4 will build. The executor becomes rustflow, and the memory model, the planner and admission are replaced by one function per step that counts and allocates. Agreed in discussion and not yet built: the code is the prototypes ../v0.0.2 and ../v0.0.3, and nothing under rust/. rust/docs/ describes the tree as it is, and changes with the implementation. There is no crate beside this page yet. Written 2026-11-25, after ../v0.0.3.

This page is the design document. It absorbed docs/rust-redesign.html ("Rust rebuild: steps, the memory budget and rustflow") on 2026-11-25, and that file is gone. From here on the latest prototype's page is the latest design: the next prototype's page carries this text forward with what changed, and an earlier prototype's page stays as it was written.

Two things were decided on 2026-11-25, and the text below has them folded in:

  1. The card owns a stream, under its lock. v0.0.3 could not build one handle set per card as the design worded it, with the handles rebound to the holder's stream. Now each card has a stream of its own, the card's lock guards that stream with the handles and the workspace, and nothing is rebound. A step owns its own streams; a step that calls the library also borrows the card's (one handle set per card).
  2. The new layer is written from scratch over the C ABI. Not over rust/'s handle wrappers, its safe kernel wrappers or its task layer, which were shaped by the memory model this design retires. The kernel libraries and the device-free crates stay (from scratch).

What v0.0.4 builds on top of that is the DF-RHF chain as steps, on num_gpus cards, batched. Like its predecessors it will be throwaway: a crate and a workspace of its own, in no gate, never merged into rust/.

The shape

A calculation is a DAG of steps, as in the C++ src/pipeline. One shared object, the MainBus, holds the dimensions, the config, the memory caps and counters, and every step's Output. A step declares which other steps' Outputs it reads; the DAG's edges are built from those declarations, and its run is handed a View of exactly them. Every step implements MemBudget: an associated function mem_budget that charges the counters for what the step will allocate and then allocates it. The same DAG runs in two modes over one rustflow Executor.

  1. Setup. Parse the config, the geometry and the bases on the host. Fills the dimensions (n_atom, n_ao, n_aux, …), with every length a step's arrays need: counted, not built. Not budgeted.
  2. Budget mode. The DAG, each node calling its step's mem_budget: count, check, allocate into the MainBus. A final node packs step scratch and accepts or refuses the whole plan.
  3. Execute mode. The same DAG, each node calling its step's run. Allocates nothing. Replays (an optimization, AIMD) write into the same buffers.

There is no planner, no table, and no byte reuse between the Outputs steps pass on. The peak of a kind is the sum of what the steps' Outputs hold, plus step scratch, plus a fixed BLAS-handle workspace. Step scratch is what dies with its step, every library call's work buffer included; it is packed into one slab per device and kind, and two steps share bytes only where the DAG already orders them, by its transitive closure. No edge is added for memory. That is the trade: some memory for a model small enough to hold in one page, and one in which work stealing cannot interact with memory at all.

Steps and the MainBus

decided The MainBus is a deliberate god object: one place that holds every step's Output. What it keeps from the C++ Pipeline is the View: a step declares the Outputs it reads, the builder makes the DAG's edges from that declaration, and run receives a view of exactly those and not the bus. A read with no edge behind it cannot be written.

decided The steps and the MainBus live in one crate. The tree's many small crates exist so that a change invalidates little of the build and test graph; the steps are the top of that graph, and invalidating them together is fine. One crate also means the bus can name every Output type with no dependency cycle.

  • An Output holds what outlives its step's run; scratch holds the rest. An Output is what other steps read, or what carries over a replay (the last geometry's orbitals as the next guess). Everything else the step allocates dies with the step and is step scratch: the DIIS rings, a Davidson subspace, a streamed pass's ring, the records and work lists, build intermediates. Revised 2026-11-21: the step's private buffers were fields of its Output, summed for the whole calculation. decided
  • A step is exactly one node of the outer graph. Something is its own step only if another step reads its product, its product carries over a replay, or it has its own config table. Everything finer is the step's inner graph, built in run: a subflow, condition loops inside it, a pipeline, or a plain loop on one stream. The SCF iteration is a condition loop inside ScfStep's subflow, not in the outer graph. Splitting a step is not free: its intermediates become Outputs, which are summed (B is the case: one step, one 7.8 GB slab, where two would hold two). What a finer step would buy, overlap between its pieces, is available inside the step. It also gives scratch one lifetime, a node's run, so the closure is over nodes. decided 2026-11-21
  • The lock is used plainly. The owning step takes write(), a reader's view takes read(). No generation stamp, no try_, no assertion. It exists because Rust will not compile shared mutation across threads without a lock or unsafe; on a chain it is never contended. A thin wrapper hides the standard lock's poisoning unwrap(). decided
  • A step reads only what it declared. The declaration is the edge, so a missing edge is a missing declaration, and that does not compile: the view has no such field. decided
  • Only the owning step writes, by visibility. An Output's mutating methods are private to its step's module. That is ordinary privacy. decided
  • A step is one config table and one slot. [gradient] is a single step as far as the TOML goes; what it computes depends on the energy method the config names. No step type has two instances, so slots need no instance key. decided
  • A step the config does not ask for is not in the DAG. A step that declares it reads one is a refusal when the DAG is built, naming both, as the C++ planner does. So no declared read finds an empty slot; the types hold only that a read is declared, and the slot's emptiness is a runtime assert (an Option budget mode fills), as the prototype found. decided
  • Shared code with per-user state is a type, not a step. DIIS is the case: the SCF's history and a coupled-cluster solver's cannot be shared and their vector lengths differ. Each user builds its own Diis in run over a slice of its step scratch (the ring of Fock and error matrices, on the device), declared in its own mem_budget. The history then lives exactly as long as the step's run, and a replay starts with none. Shared code over a shared product is the same thing with a View added (below). decided 2026-11-23
  • An Output can be loaded from a file, in part or whole, as in src/pipeline. That is how a step is tested alone: its inputs are loaded and not computed. Budget mode still allocates; the load fills. decided
  • Replay allocates nothing. A geometry optimization or an AIMD trajectory reruns the chain at the same dimensions, so each step writes again into the buffers budget mode made. What must not carry over between replays (a DIIS history, a converged flag) has to be cleared by someone; what should (the last geometry's orbitals as the next guess) must not be. Three candidates: a prologue node at the loop's entry, an epilogue node at its end, or a reset mode that some steps support. The prototype's reset mode sufficed; one way to make what carries over explicit is a trait method the wrapper calls when a replay enters (an on_replay with a no-op default). Tagged for later, 2026-11-21. The split above narrows it: what lives in scratch never carries over, so only an Output's fields need the rule. A DIIS history is scratch, so it is no longer part of the question. to discuss
  • mem_budget is an associated function. Sizes depend on step options (nroots, a Davidson subspace) as well as on the dimensions, so it reads both from the MainBus.

Shared code: a function over a View, not a manager

decided 2026-11-23: there are no managers. The earlier form made EriStep's Output an EriManager: an object owning B, the machinery over it, scratch of its own and a lock around that scratch. Once the scratch moves to the callers, nothing mutable is left inside, and what remains is an Output and a function.

  • A shared product is an ordinary Output. In density-fitting mode EriOutput holds the metric (P|Q) and its factor L on every card, and B. Steps read it through their declared View, like any other. Two steps may read it at once: each holds a read view.

  • The J and K build is a function. It takes a View of B, the density, the J and K to write (the caller's), a slice of the caller's scratch, and the guards of the cards its caller holds (the lock): build_jk(&b, &density, &mut j, &mut k, scratch). It has no state of its own, so no internal lock and no question about concurrent callers. DIIS is the same without the View (above).

  • The callee gives the size, the caller declares it. A size function (jk_scratch(dims, n_dens)), pure in Dims and the options, with a row per card; each caller adds it to its own declaration in mem_budget, and the build works in the caller's region, since it runs during the caller's run. Packing then covers one density for the SCF, nroots for a state average and many right-hand sides for a response solve with no new rule. The closure model checked both forms with two unordered callers: declared by the callers, safe in every simulated run; owned by the callee, one region used during both callers' runs, a collision in every one of 300.

  • It writes only what it is passed. J and K belong to the caller. More generally, global mutable statics are minimized and, ideally, banned.

  • Whoever fans out, joins. A function that recruits every card owns the whole of that: each inner task makes its card current with a ScopedDeviceIndex, the fragments each card produces (not necessarily disjoint) are reduced onto gpu0, and the call returns with that done. The caller sees an ordinary call. The caller is still an all-GPU step: the scratch and the streams on every card are its own.

  • One B, built over pairs, contracted over aux functions. The two loops run over different indices, as experimental/rust/df_batched/single_gpu builds it:

    • The build runs over batches of orbital shell pairs (mn). A column mn of B depends on every P, so a batch of P cannot be fitted alone; a batch of pairs can. Each card takes its own batches, forms (mn|P), fits it against its own copy of L, and downloads the batch to the host. No card needs anything from another.
    • B's home is the host heap, one slab of EriOutput. The cards hold the O(N²) data and one batch each, which is scratch. So host memory is what B is held against, not gpu0's cap.
    • The J and K build runs over batches of P. Each card takes its own batches from the host, contracts them into fragments of J and K, and the fragments are summed onto gpu0.

    A second copy of B is ruled out by size.

  • Derivatives of B are scratch. Analytic gradients, Hessians and higher derivatives build the derivative of B a batch at a time and contract it at once, inside the step that needs it. It is never an Output; its records and work lists are that step's scratch too. Those steps read the metric and L, so the two are real Outputs and not, as an earlier draft had it, products only B reads.

  • Batches of B kept on the cards between passes. Streaming uploads all of B on every J and K pass (4.3 s on gly10 in the experiment, B on the heap). A cache over the host copy would keep as many batches on each card as the step's limit allows and stream the rest. An optimization for after the streamed path works. proposed

The memory budget

decided The names: MemBudget the trait, mem_budget its function, budget mode the pass that runs it, Budget the handle it allocates through. footprint and layout are dropped. The C++ footprint returned a description that tests held in step with the allocation; here the description is the allocation, so there is still one program.

pub trait MemBudget {
    /// Charge what this step allocates, allocate it, and put the
    /// `Output` in the bus. Budget mode calls it once per step.
    fn mem_budget(bus: &MainBus, budget: &Budget);
}

impl MemBudget for ScfStep {
    fn mem_budget(bus: &MainBus, budget: &Budget) {
        let n = bus.dims.n_ao;
        let dev = bus.device_of::<Self>();
        let output = ScfOutput {
            c:    budget.device(dev, n * n),      // summed: the CASSCF reads it
            eps:  budget.heap(n),                 // a fixed-size host slab
            ..
        };
        // Dies with the run: the Fock matrix, the DIIS ring, the eigensolve's
        // work. One region, packed by the final node, lent to run.
        budget.scratch(dev, Kind::Device,
            n * n + diis_bytes(bus) + syevd_lwork(n) + eri_jk_scratch(bus, 1));
        *bus.scf.write() = Some(output);
    }
}

A sketch, not a signature to hold anyone to. The step's streams are not in it: the wrapper makes them and keeps them beside the Output in the slot, so it can sync them without the step's cooperation (as the prototype found). What is fixed is what each Budget call for step-owned memory does, in order:

  1. Add the bytes to the kind's atomic counter (per device for device and pinned memory), and write them into this step's row of a per-step table.
  2. Check the counter against the cap. Over it: set a shared over_budget flag. Do not panic here.
  3. Allocate, unless the flag is set.

A budget.scratch call only records a row (step, device, kind, bytes); it charges and allocates nothing. The final node of the budget DAG then: takes the closure of the DAG and packs each device and kind's scratch rows into one slab (step scratch); adds each packed peak and the fixed BLAS-handle workspace (below) to its counter and makes the same check; holds the heap and pinned totals against the host's available memory; and, if the flag is set, prints the whole table with the totals and exits. Otherwise it allocates the scratch slabs and each card's handle workspace, binds the latter to that card's BLAS handle, once (below), records each step's offset, and logs the table.

  • One pass, not a count pass and an allocate pass. Two modes in all: budget and execute. decided
  • The same DAG, in parallel, with atomic counters. A serial pass would be a different execution graph. The total is order-independent, so accept or refuse is the same every run; deferring the exit to the final node is what makes the report the same every run too, complete and not whichever step crossed the line. The only waste on a refusal is what was allocated before the flag flipped, and the process is exiting. decided
  • The device check has two moments: per step for Outputs, and once at the end for the packed scratch and the handle workspace. A plan can pass every step and be refused at the end; the table carries each as its own row so that reads plainly. decided
  • Running the passes in parallel earns its keep on the host, where touching the pages of a multi-GB slab is the slow part.

Lengths: counted in setup, built by the step

A kernel cannot walk a BasisSet. It reads flat arrays that say, for every unit of work, where its inputs are and where its result goes; the host builds them from the geometry and the bases, stages them in pinned memory and uploads them. They are of two kinds. Records are data: one entry per primitive, with its centre, exponent and weighted coefficient. Work lists are indices: one entry per unit of kernel work.

decided 2026-11-21: no umbrella name. "Manifest" named them while they were a budget category of their own, counted in setup, charged in mem_budget, built in run and kept for the whole calculation. A step rebuilds them on every run, so keeping them buys nothing: they are step scratch, pinned for the staging and device for what the kernels read, and once their step is done their bytes go to later steps. What is left of the category is one rule, about lengths, and it covers every array a step allocates, Output or scratch. A binding that launches over records and work lists may name a struct holding both locally; the prototype's Tables collides with the budget table and goes.

decided 2026-11-21: setup counts, the step builds.

  • Setup fills every length. It has already read the geometry and both bases, and it is outside the DAG and unbudgeted, so it adds the counts the arrays need to Dims: n_aux_prim, n_aux_shell and n_shell_pair beside the n_col it already derives from the orbital RecordTables. Counting is a walk over the shells and builds nothing. A mem_budget is then a function of Dims and the step's options alone, as the step contract says.
  • Setup counts every length that scales; it builds none of those arrays. The rule for what setup may do: counts, and small host data the dimensions need. Any array whose length scales with a pair count, or a product of shell counts, has its length counted into Dims by setup, and belongs to a step and is budgeted: B's slab as much as the work lists. cells is the case: 1.79 GB on gly10, which would otherwise sit on the plain heap with nothing counting it.
  • mem_budget charges, run builds. mem_budget declares the records and work lists at their lengths from Dims as scratch, pinned and device. It builds nothing. run builds them straight into its pinned region (a counting pass over the shells gives each class's offset, so no temporary Vec is needed), uploads them on the step's stream into its device region, and launches.
  • A replay builds again, into the same regions. The records hold coordinates, which move in an optimization or a trajectory; the lengths do not, and neither do the offsets. So every run rebuilds in place and allocates nothing. Rebuilding only the records (the work lists depend on the bases alone) is to measure.
  • A refused or dry budget builds nothing and still prints exact lengths, since they are counts. This is what the prototype could not do: its Tables::new ran in mem_budget for the lengths and again in run for the contents, because a refused or dry budget hands back empty buffers it could not fill.
  • Not a panic on the first over-cap charge. That would fix the refused case only (a dry run's buffers are empty by design) and would undo the decision above: in a parallel budget pass which step crosses the cap first is scheduling, so the report would name a different step from run to run instead of the whole table.

Host heap

  • A boxed slice, not a Vec. Box<[T]> has no push, reserve or extend, so "no growing" is the type's property and needs no lint. Everything taking &[T] or &mut [T] works unchanged. The HeapSlab newtype is constructible only by Budget, which separates budgeted memory from an incidental Vec in a signature. decided
  • Touch the pages in mem_budget. Zeroed memory is handed out lazily on Linux, so an untouched slab "succeeds" without existing and fails at first touch mid-run, as a bare kill from the OOM killer. decided
  • The host is checked at every call, and again by the final node. Heap and pinned bytes add to one host counter, held against the host cap at each Budget call, before the slab is allocated and its pages touched: a B larger than the host sets the flag and is never touched, where a check in the final node alone would come after the OOM killer. The final node repeats the check with the packed pinned scratch added. The default cap is the cgroup-aware reading that exists today (fb_core_arena::host_available), taken once in setup, less its margin (caps). decided 2026-11-24; the first form checked in the final node only.

Step scratch

decided 2026-11-21. Memory a step needs during its run and that nothing reads afterwards: the build intermediates of B, which were 68% of the prototype's eri device bytes on benzene (237 of 347 MiB); the work buffer of every library call (syevd's at order n, …; below); a solver's DIIS ring or Davidson subspace; and the records and work lists, staged in pinned memory and read on the device. Without it the only home for them is the step's Output, summed for the whole calculation.

  • Why not allocate and free it inside the step. Two reasons. Allocation in run is ruled out (Enforcement), and a replay would allocate again, which breaks "a replay allocates nothing" and lets a replay fail late. And under the sum, a release frees bytes no step can use: admission happened up front and counted every step's bytes for the whole run, so what eri gives back mid-run was promised to nobody. What lowers the peak is sharing scratch between steps, not releasing it.
  • Declared per step, packed by the closure. A step declares budget.scratch(dev, kind, bytes) in mem_budget, for device or pinned memory; the call records a row and allocates nothing. The final budget node takes the transitive closure of the DAG (a bitset of successors per node, at a few dozen nodes) and packs each device and kind's rows into one slab: two steps' regions may overlap only if one reaches the other. It packs by first fit, each region at the lowest offset clear of every unordered region already placed, in a fixed order, so the offsets, and the table, are the same every run. Then it allocates one slab per device and kind and records each step's offset.
  • The order: largest first. First fit by size, then step id, and no other order. The closure model found it at the lower bound on 98.1% of random DAGs and 1.281× the bound at worst; the best of four fixed orders reaches 99.2% and 1.174×, and is not taken. A poorer packing costs bytes and never safety (next item), real pipelines are mostly chains, and the budget table prints the lower bound beside the packed peak, so a config that packs badly shows itself. More orders are added when one does. decided 2026-11-24
  • The closure alone, not the card's lock. Two steps that both hold a card's lock (below) never run at once on it either, so they could share scratch there with no path between them. The packer does not use that: unordered regions stay apart whoever holds the lock. One safety argument and one test, for the bytes of the smaller of two unordered library users (in DF-RHF, the orthogonalizer's eigensolve work, 8.7 MB on gly10). decided 2026-11-24
  • Safe whatever the packer's quality. Safety is the packer's constraint, not its objective: any placement that keeps unordered regions apart is safe, the best and the worst alike. A poorer packing costs bytes, and at worst refuses a plan a better one would have admitted: a refusal at init, never a collision in a run.
  • Why the closure is enough. A finished node is finished device work (the wrapper's step-end sync), so a path in the DAG from A to B is a happens-before on the card as well as on the host: B cannot touch the bytes before A is done with them, under any schedule the executor picks. Unordered steps never share. So, unlike the old assigner, which made reuse possible by adding handoff edges, nothing here constrains the schedule, and work stealing still cannot interact with memory. The model's simulated runs on a stealing pool agree, and its negative control shows the sync is what carries it (below).
  • The step-end sync now carries memory safety. It was introduced to order the card between dependent steps (streams); with scratch packed, it is also what keeps bytes shared along a path from being used twice at once. Pinned scratch needs both of its halves: the host writes it inside run, before the node finishes, and the device reads it through copies on the step's streams, done by the time the wrapper's sync returns. So a cheaper replacement is not free. Event chaining (each step's stream waiting on its predecessors' join events, forwarded by host-only nodes) still orders device work along every path, which keeps device scratch safe; it does not order a host write in B after a copy A enqueued from the same pinned bytes. Any change to the sync is a change to the scratch rule, and is decided with it.
  • Computed once. The graph and every length are fixed for the calculation, so the closure and the offsets are made once in budget mode and every replay runs in the same regions. A replayed chain's closure is taken over one iteration of its body, laid flat into the outer DAG with the steps before and after it: a module cannot run concurrently with itself, so one iteration ends before the next begins, two steps unordered within the body stay apart, as they must, and so does a step outside the loop that no path orders against it. The model packs once and runs three iterations unrolled: safe statically and in simulated runs.
  • A condition's exclusive branches are packed as unordered. Two branches of which only one runs never use their regions at once, so they could share bytes. The closure does not know that, and keeps them apart: safe, and the branches' sum rather than their max (in the model, 220 against 120 MiB). Packing them as exclusive is also safe, and is not adopted: a choice made in the config never enters the DAG, and a runtime condition, such as a convergence loop, lives inside a step's subflow, so exclusive branches of the outer graph should be rare. decided
  • Over a replay the addresses hold and the contents do not. A step gets the same region at the same address on every run. What is in it on entry is undefined, on the first run and on every replay: any step the DAG orders against it may have written those bytes in between, and nothing clears them. A step whose region happens to share with nobody would find its old data still there, and must not rely on it. The check: in a test mode the wrapper fills each lease with NaN before run, so a step that depends on what was left fails in the gate. decided 2026-11-23
  • One layout function per step. A step's region holds several tenants (the SCF's: the DIIS ring, the J and K build's scratch, the eigensolve's work). mem_budget sums their sizes and run carves the region, so two pieces of code must agree. One function from Dims and the options to a struct of offsets serves both: mem_budget declares its total, run slices by it. decided 2026-11-24
  • A lease that cannot escape. The wrapper hands run a lease on its regions that borrows for the length of run, not 'static. A step cannot store its scratch in its Output, so "scratch dies with the step" holds by type. The overlapping slices need one unsafe, in the arena, with the closure invariant written beside it.
  • A step's inner tasks split its region themselves. The closure is over the outer graph's nodes, a step being one node; two unordered tasks of one step's subflow share bytes only if the step arranges it. Inner graphs are small and written by hand, so the closure is not taken recursively.
  • What it keeps. Everything allocated in budget mode; nothing allocated in execute mode; accept or refuse independent of order; no lock, so a scratch-using step never waits on another.
  • What it costs. Bytes in place of time. The packed peak is at least the largest single declaration, and equals it only when every scratch user is chained. In DF-RHF, eri and the orthogonalizer are unordered, so their regions are disjoint; the SCF follows both and may reuse either. Revised 2026-11-21: the first form was one max per device under a lock, which made eri and X serialize. Since 2026-11-24 the two serialize anyway, as library users under gpu0's lock (below); the regions stay disjoint, by the item on the closure above. Unordered steps that call no library still overlap on the card.
  • The host heap is not packed. It stays a sum. What a step's run needs on the heap and drops is small, or it is a pinned staging buffer, which is scratch.
  • Small host scratch is a policy, not a kind. A Vec or a HashMap that lives within a step's run is assumed small, and is documented as outside the budget; there is no host scratch kind and no suballocator. What holds the policy is the allocator record of Enforcement, which flags any single large allocation during run in the test tiers. One number added to it: the peak heap growth over execute mode, printed as an unbudgeted row of the budget table. The rule a step is written to: heap scratch in run stays within O(n_ao²), and anything larger is a HeapSlab on the MainBus. Every large object of the methods here has a length that is a closed form in the dimensions, so none needs to live outside the bus; what the record is for is the accident (cells was one). A host suballocator with the standard collections banned was raised again on 2026-11-24 and not taken: allocator-aware collections are not on stable Rust, so it would mean rewriting every host routine over slices, to hold a rule the record already checks by size. decided 2026-11-24
  • A batch bounds the scratch. Every build over an O(N³) tensor is batched, so its scratch is one batch on each card it runs on, and an all-GPU step declares a row per card.
  • The same machinery would cover Outputs. An Output only one later step reads still holds its bytes for the whole calculation. Packing it too is the same closure over a different interval, from its producer to its last reader. Held until the measurement (open questions) says Outputs, not scratch, are what stacks. to discuss

Caps, and what the limit covers

  • Caps come from the user's config, per kind, as today. decided
  • The reserve measurement is dropped: the per-worker reading, the 256 MiB floor, the growth tracking. Its job was to predict a failure that would otherwise come late; with everything allocated in budget mode a real out-of-memory surfaces at init, where the tree's own abort names it. decided
  • An explicit device cap is used as written. The user owns the margin. With no cap given, the default is the device's free memory at start less a margin: one read per device, after its handles exist, which also sees another process already holding part of a shared card. decided
  • The device margin is the larger of 512 MiB and 5% of the card's total memory. It is for what the card takes after budget mode (below): the kernels' stack, and modules loading at first launch. Both numbers are judgment and not measurement; they scale with the card because the stack does. No scan of the kernels and no test ties the constant to them. One that turns out too small costs a failed launch that says so (failure), and is raised then; a config that wants the memory back sets an explicit cap. decided 2026-11-24
  • The host cap has the same default. With none given, it is the host's available memory read once in setup, less a margin, and every heap and pinned charge is held against it as it is made (host heap). With B on the host heap this is the cap a large density-fitted run meets first. decided 2026-11-24
  • The host margin is the larger of 1 GiB and 5% of the available memory. It tracks nothing. Host scratch is outside the budget by policy (step scratch), and only what is on the MainBus is counted; the margin is room left unpromised, so that a B admitted up to the whole of the host cannot leave the uncounted allocations of a run with nothing, which would end as a kill from the OOM killer mid-run. Judgment again: the policy holds a step's host scratch within O(n_ao²), megabytes at these sizes. decided 2026-11-24
  • Every cap is clamped to what the machine has at init. A plan is held against the smaller of the cap and the reading made at start: each card's free memory once its handles exist, and the host's available memory in setup. A defaulted cap is that reading less its margin; an explicit one past the machine is clamped to the reading itself, with no margin taken, since the user owns it. The budget table prints the cap as written beside the value used. Without the clamp an explicit cap the machine cannot meet is not a refusal: on a card the tree aborts at whichever allocation fails first, with no table and a different step named from run to run, and on the host budget mode touches pages until the OOM killer ends the process with no message. The reading is a snapshot, so on a shared machine it can refuse a plan that would have fitted a moment later; the refusal prints what it read. decided 2026-11-24

The compliance statement, to be written into the user docs whatever else changes decided: the memory limits cover what steps allocate through mem_budget, step scratch (which holds every library call's work buffer), and the fixed BLAS-handle workspace. They exclude the device context, the handles' own memory and loaded modules, the kernels' stack, and small host allocations such as reading the geometry and the basis data. A defaulted cap leaves a margin for those; an explicit one is the user's.

What a card takes after budget mode

decided 2026-11-24: none of it is budgeted, measured or checked at run time. The margin covers it, and the failure it leaves is loud and lands in one place.

  • The kernels' stack is wholly outside the budget. CUDA holds a kernel's per-thread stack for every thread the card can keep resident (104,448 on the RTX 3080), from that kernel's first launch on, by raising the device's stack limit and keeping it (src/core/wwr/README.md, "Kernel stacks outside the pool"). At the largest frame the Rust-only kernels have, 3,856 bytes in their low-stack form, that is about 280 MiB on the 3080; at the C++ tree's largest, 16,912 bytes, 1.6 GiB. It has no row in the budget table and no step declares it.
  • What that risks. One failure: on CUDA, a plan that nearly fills the card fails at the first launch of a kernel whose frame is larger than any launched so far, with an allocation error. If the kernel is a late step's, the run loses the work before it. Nothing is silent and no result is wrong. It is isolable here in a way it was not under the old model, where the driver trimmed a pool's cached memory to make the stack and the failure surfaced at a later, unrelated allocation (#1259): there is no pool, everything budgeted is allocated before the first launch, and so the failure can only be the launch itself, in a named step. It needs free memory after budget mode below the stack the run will take, which a defaulted cap's margin rules out while the margin is the larger.
  • Nothing on HIP. ROCm sizes a kernel's scratch per dispatch and holds no reservation sized by the frame: no growth past the first launch at 1 KiB, 10,000 or 23,200 bytes of scratch a lane, and a 23,200-byte launch ran on a card full but for 256 MiB (gfx1200, ROCm 7.2.4, the same README). What a large launch takes while it runs was not sampled, and the measurement predates ROCm 10 (to measure).
  • Not taken: claiming it. A row of the budget, counted under the cap and sized per step, needs each kernel's frame. That is known when the kernel is compiled and can be read from the built objects on both backends (cuobjdump --dump-resource-usage, as the C++ ctest core_kernel_stack_bound does; on HIP .private_segment_fixed_size in the code object's metadata note, by llvm-readelf --notes once the object is taken out of .hip_fatbin). So it would be a generated manifest of frames, a declaration in every step, and a device-attribute call wwr's C ABI does not have, for the card's thread count: all for one backend, to make an explicit cap honest about one of several things the compliance statement (above) already excludes, and to save at most the difference between the tree's largest frame and the run's.
  • Not taken: raising the stack limit at init. One wwr_device_set_limit to a tree-wide constant, before the reading the default cap is made from, would take the stack before anything is budgeted and move the failure to init. It needs that constant kept at the largest frame the layer launches, by hand or by a scan of the compiled kernels, and the margin covers the case without either. It is the known fix, about ten lines, if a late failure ever costs a real run.
  • When to look again. When a kernel with a frame past 8 KiB is ported (the Hessian kernels are the ones): the stack then outgrows the margin on a small card, and both of the above are worth their cost.
  • Modules and a library's first call are the margin's too. Kernel modules load at first launch, and a library takes a few MiB at its first call (2–10 MiB at a HIP solver handle's, up to 14 on cuSOLVER, by the probes). No eager loading, no warm-up launch and no warm-up call in budget mode. The handles themselves (10–14 MiB each on CUDA, 58–60 on HIP) exist before the free-memory reading, so the default cap already sees them.

What is given up, and what disappears

Given up: byte reuse between Outputs. Every step's Output stays allocated for the run; down a chain, they stack. Reuse within a step is untouched: a step owns its buffers and may use one for two purposes, so where two things should share bytes, the tool is the step boundary (the example has the case that matters, B solved in place). Past that, the remedy when a calculation does not fit is a narrower batch in the step concerned, not a shared pool. The exception is step scratch: memory dead after a step's run, library work buffers included, packed per device and kind so that steps the DAG orders share bytes.

Reuse and batching close different gaps. There are three numbers per calculation: the sum (every buffer alive at once), the fold (the peak with bytes reused perfectly) and the cap. Reuse narrows sum toward fold and can never go below it; batching lowers the fold itself. So where a chain's fold fits and its sum does not, packing is the remedy; where the peak sits inside one step (DF-HF's is inside eri: a batch, its records and work lists, and its build intermediates, all live at once), only that step's own design helps: its batch width, or a kernel that derives its work lists. Packing scratch alone reaches part of the way from sum to fold; how far depends on how much of each step is scratch.

Disappears:

  • The planner, the table, per-buffer lifetimes, the assigner and its handoff edges. What lifetime there is, a step's run, comes from the DAG as written.
  • "The peak under stealing". Bytes are shared only between steps the DAG orders, so no order of execution can break memory, and the bytes-against-concurrency search is moot.
  • The adaptive pass and the question of iterating it.
  • The reserve, its floor and its growth.
  • Nested regions: a replay reuses its own buffers.
  • The plan goldens' table totals; what is pinned instead is the budget table.

What can still be refused (revised 2026-11-23, with batched only): a plan whose O(N²) data does not fit its cards, whose B does not fit the host, or whose Outputs, each fitting alone, do not fit summed down a chain. The last is the sum's price. The ratio is worth knowing: the C++ footprints already give, for each examples/ config, the sum of the steps' own peaks against the fold's peak. Split three ways, it says which remedy applies: each step's Output bytes, its scratch bytes, and whether the peak sits inside one step or across many. It is information, with no bar attached.

Work stealing: what the one thread was providing

A GPU worker today is one thread that never waits on the device. Four things rest on that, and each needs an explicit owner once any rustflow worker may run any step.

  1. The current device. Launches and library calls run on the calling thread's current device. The step's wrapper makes its device current for the body (the C++ rule for a taskflow task: a ScopedDeviceIndex in the task), and so does every inner task that launches, since a stolen inner task runs on a thread whose current device is whatever it last used. Not run: the experiment bound one device per worker thread, once.
  2. Exclusive use of the library handles. Enqueueing on a stream is thread-safe on both runtimes; the handle is the question. cuBLAS documents calls on a shared handle as thread-safe and warns only about reconfiguring it under other users; rocBLAS says not to share a handle between threads (verify both). The card's lock, held by one step at a time (below), answers it either way.
  3. Order between unordered steps. It was repeatable and now varies run to run. Correctness never rested on it, provided every ordering that matters is an edge. run with lanes kept and chained: results bitwise equal.
  4. Order on the card between dependent steps. One lane's stream gave it for free. Here the step's own sync gives it: a finished node means finished device work.

Device assignment is fixed before budget mode decided: mem_budget has to know which device's counter to charge, and an all-GPU step charges every card. The assignment is made when the DAG is built from the config. Stealing moves which thread runs a step, never where its memory is.

The rule of assignment decided 2026-11-23: the config gives num_gpus, and a step runs on no GPU, on gpu0, or on all of them. Those are the only three; no step runs on card k alone, or on some of the cards.

  • gpu0 is the privileged card. A one-GPU step runs on gpu0. A card has to be privileged in any case, since dense diagonalization is single-GPU for now. The overlap, computed on the host as experimental/rust/s_overlap_host does, is uploaded to gpu0 and nowhere else.
  • An all-GPU step uses every card and splits its data across them. Anything that builds J, or contracts like it, is one: the batches are shared out, each card works on its own, and the result is reduced onto gpu0. A step that calls an all-GPU function is an all-GPU step (the SCF, through the J and K build), though its eigensolve still runs on gpu0.
  • With one GPU, all is gpu0. A single card is the degenerate case of the same code, not a second path.
  • A device buffer is on gpu0 or on every card. Which is said by the Budget call that allocates it, not by the step's kind: the SCF is an all-GPU step whose orbitals are on gpu0; the metric and its factor are on every card (experimental/rust/df_metric's (P|Q), uploaded to each). The one rule joining the two: an all-GPU step's reduced results land on gpu0.
  • So gpu0's cap is the one that binds: every one-GPU step's memory and every reduced result is charged there, and the other cards hold their copies of the every-card buffers and their batches.
  • "Target" is dropped. It named the card a product lives on, so that a one-GPU step could run on card k and deliver to gpu0, and a reader on another card could be refused. With three kinds of step and two places for a buffer, neither case exists: nothing is delivered, and no reader is on the wrong card. Revised 2026-11-23; the earlier form (2026-11-21) had "the producer delivers to the target, and a reader never pulls".

One handle set per card, under one lock

The design. decided 2026-11-25. It revises the form of 2026-11-24, which kept no stream in CardResources and rebound the handles to the holder's stream at every acquisition of the lock (SetStream, then SetWorkspace).

  • The card owns a stream. Each card's CardResources holds one stream, a BLAS handle and a dense-solver handle made on that stream, and the fixed 128 MiB BLAS workspace. The handles keep that stream for the life of the process. Nothing is rebound: SetStream is never called after creation, and the workspace is set once.
  • The lock guards all of it. CardResources is a Mutex around the stream, the handles and the workspace, not a Mutex<()> beside them. They are reachable only through the guard. A further library object a step needs on that card (a sparse or FFT handle, a collectives communicator) joins the same set under the same lock, so there is never an order between locks to get wrong.
  • A step owns its streams. A GPU step has streams of its own on each card it spans: one by default, more if it declares them. The wrapper makes them in budget mode and keeps them in the step's slot.
  • A library-using step also borrows the card's. It declares the library per card in mem_budget, beside its scratch. For its run it holds that card's guard, and its library calls go on the card's stream.
  • The wrapper takes the guards, and syncs before it drops them. Before run it takes the guard of every card the step declared, in card order, so two multi-card steps cannot deadlock, and that order also keeps collectives issued in the same order on every rank. After run it synchronizes every stream the step has on each card, and each held card's stream, and only then drops the guards. A step re-run by a condition loop takes the lock each time.
  • Safety is the scratch argument, unchanged. A finished node is finished device work on every stream the step touched, the card's included, so the closure still orders every byte two steps share. The previous holder of a card's handles has synced the card's stream before releasing them, so no handle is ever shared with work in flight, and no two steps ever use the fixed workspace at once.
  • The BLAS workspace is fixed, 128 MiB per card, a row of the budget table (workspace). Solver work buffers stay per call, sized by their query, in step scratch: syevd's grows as n² (130 MiB at n = 2048 on cuSOLVER), so no fixed size covers them. rocBLAS's trsm, the one BLAS call in the same position, is not called: the triangular solve is the tree's recipe, which needs only the fixed workspace (workspace).
  • A step's inner tasks share its handle set. An inner task for a card takes that card's guard from the step, mutably, so two inner tasks cannot call the library at once on one card (rocBLAS says not to share a handle between threads; verify). A step that wants concurrent library calls inside itself declares further handles of its own, like scratch.

Why not the rebind. v0.0.3 set out to build the 2026-11-24 form and could not, on rust/ as merged (its README, "What the prototype says to change", item 1):

  • fb_core_wwr::blas::BlasHandle keeps the Arc<Stream> it was made on and has no rebind.
  • fb_recipes_sys::trsm launches its diagonal kernels on that stream (blas.owner()) and its gemms through the handle. A handle rebound through the raw call would run one solve on two streams with nothing ordering them.
  • BlasHandle::bind_workspace takes ownership of the workspace, where the design's workspace is the card's and outlives every binding.

So v0.0.3 made a BLAS handle per library-using step per card: the form rejected below for its cost on HIP. Those three are properties of rust/'s wrappers and not of the libraries, and a layer written from scratch has none of them: a rebind could be built there. It is still not taken, for the one reason that is the libraries' own. The rebind is safe by measurement, on one release of each: cuBLAS drops the workspace at SetStream, hipBLAS keeps it (below), and what the solver handles do was not measured.

What it buys.

  • Safe by construction. With no rebind there is nothing to measure about SetStream, and no test of it to keep across toolkit bumps.
  • Exclusive use by type. The handles are behind the guard, so "no handle is shared with work in flight" is the borrow checker's.
  • A recipe cannot be handed the wrong stream. The library bindings and the recipes take the card's guard: the stream and the handles together, in place of a handle and a stream passed apart. trsm's diagonal kernels and its gemms land on one stream because the guard has only one, and a step with streams of its own has no way to pass one of them.
  • The default cap sees the handles. They are made in setup, so "one read per device, after its handles exist" holds as caps words it, and their memory does not grow with the number of library-using steps.

What it gives up: overlap between library-using steps the DAG leaves unordered on one card. The lock is held through a step's non-library phases too. In DF-RHF's setup that is eri against the orthogonalizer: the orthogonalizer's one syevd at n_ao, a few milliseconds, waits for eri. So library-using steps are serial on a card, whatever the DAG says; steps that call no library still overlap. The packer takes nothing from this: two lock holders the DAG leaves unordered keep disjoint scratch (step scratch). Heavy steps fill the card on their own, and two of them on one card time-slice rather than overlap: this tree's test batches measured a CASSCF case at 2.3 s alone and 158 s beside the exchange build.

Given up with it since 2026-11-25: narrowing the lock to a step's library phases later. A holder's library calls are on the card's stream, so two library-using steps cannot interleave on a card without a second handle set. The lock was already held through a step's whole run, and the ring of handle sets already declined, so nothing decided is lost.

Not taken.

  • A rebind, as above: a handle moved to the holder's stream at every acquisition, with the recipes reading the stream from the current binding. It keeps every step's library calls on the step's own stream, at the price of resting on the measured SetStream behaviour.
  • Every recipe takes its stream explicitly. It does not remove the rebind; it only moves the stream out of the handle.
  • Rebinding inside a step, per call, to each of its streams. Two library calls in flight on two streams would share the one fixed workspace.
  • A ring of handle sets, assigned by the closure so that two steps share a set only if one reaches the other. It keeps that overlap, at a handle set per unit of the DAG's width (about 20 MiB on CUDA and 52 on HIP, measured below) and an assignment in the final budget node, for overlap the paragraph above says is worth little.
  • A handle set per step. The same with no sharing, at a set per library-using step: ten such steps cost 520 MiB on HIP against 52. It is what v0.0.3 built, for want of a rebind.
  • The lock per call. It waits less, but two unordered steps would then have calls in flight on one handle at once, and whether cuSOLVER or rocBLAS keep internal device state that those calls would race on is unverified.
  • The join made by the access: the old library stream kept, a step reaching the handles only through a call that forks from its stream and joins back. It closes the hole, but every simple library user then pays a fork and a join per call, where the lock's holder pays none.

No free-memory check at run time decided 2026-11-24. It was proposed here: in execute mode nothing allocates, so a card's free memory should hold its value from the end of budget mode, and one memory-info read by the wrapper after each step's sync would catch what a vendor library takes by itself, which the compile-time rule (Enforcement) cannot see. Dropped, in every form:

  • What it was for is ruled out by construction. Every case it was written for was a vendor call taking memory by itself: rocBLAS's trsm, and potrs and getrs on HIP, which are no longer called (workspace); a workspace too small on rocBLAS, which the fixed 128 MiB, bound once, covers; a null work buffer, which no binding passes. What is left is a toolkit bump changing what a call takes, or a call nobody measured.
  • The reading moves for reasons that are not the tree's. The kernels' stack at a first launch on CUDA, modules loading, a library's first call (what a card takes after budget mode), and any other process on the card. As an error it needs an allowance for each, or a warm-up launch per kernel family and a warm-up call per library in budget mode; as a warning it is mostly noise.
  • It is weakest where it would be enforced. The gates run small molecules, where growth that scales with a call's operands is the size of the allocator's noise; it would show on production runs, where it could only warn.
  • A replay test is not taken either: the card's free memory equal across replays 2 to n, in the gates. It needs no allowance, since the stack and the modules are loaded by then, but anything else on the card moves it, so it is a flaky gate or one with a tolerance; and what it would catch, a vendor call that leaks per call, is the sentinel test's (workspace, proposed), at the binding.
  • What is accepted. A vendor call that starts taking memory after a toolkit bump, on a shape no test runs, surfaces as an out-of-memory failure on a large run. It is loud, and it is at a library call in a named step.

What the measurements showed run 2026-11-24, RTX 3080 and RX 9060 XT, through wwr-sys (experimental/rust/handle_ring, whose README has every reading):

  1. A further handle is not nearly free. Each BLAS handle after the first takes 8 MiB on cuBLAS and 58 on hipBLAS, each solver handle 12 on cuSOLVER and 58 on hipSOLVER; on HIP each gives back rocBLAS's 32 MiB pool once it has a caller's workspace. So a handle set costs about 20 MiB on CUDA and 52 on HIP, and the first-call growth (10 on HIP, 0 to 14 on cuSOLVER) is once per process. A handle per step costs the pipeline's length times that; one set per card is the floor.
  2. The move is safe in the proposal's order. The design no longer moves a handle (2026-11-25); these readings stand as what a rebind would rest on. On cuBLAS a call made after SetStream then SetWorkspace used the workspace it was given; with the two reversed, SetStream silently put the handle back on cuBLAS's own workspace, outside the budget, with no error (read from the result of a split-K gemm whose algorithm follows the workspace length). On hipBLAS SetStream keeps the workspace, so the order does not matter there (read from a dtrsm at the DF fit's shape, which writes 8 MiB of the workspace in every order). Nothing is taken by a move on either backend, and an idle switch costs 0.02 µs on CUDA and 0.15 on HIP; the first SetWorkspace of a hipBLAS handle costs about 150 µs, the pool's free, once per handle at budget time. The two calls belong in one wrapper function that nothing else reorders.
  3. On HIP a workspace too small grows. Given 256 B, that dtrsm took 8 MiB of its own at the call, outside the budget, with no error; cuBLAS only changes algorithm. So on HIP the card's workspace must cover the largest BLAS call any step makes: hence 128 MiB. Measured since (the workspace scaling probe): it covers every call but trsm, whose need grows with its right-hand sides, and which is no longer called (workspace).

A step with streams of its own and library calls

decided 2026-11-25. experimental/rust/df_batched/multi_gpu_spherical is the case: each card builds its share of B over a ring of two batches in flight, and every trip has kernels, a library solve and a download. v0.0.4's eri is built that way, so the rule above is run in the form that needs events.

Per card, with a ring of depth 2:

  • The ring's slots are the step's own streams, one per slot. A slot's stream carries its batch's table upload, its three-centre kernels and its download.
  • The fit goes on the card's stream. It waits on the event the slot recorded after its kernels, and the slot's download waits on the event recorded after the fit.
  • The host waits on a slot's last event, not on a whole stream, before it scatters that batch into B and writes the slot's pinned bytes again.
  • The metric's factor is on the card's stream before the loop, and overlaps the first batch's kernels.

The events are a step's own inner fan-out. Whoever fans out, joins: every event is waited on before run returns, and the wrapper's sync is the last word.

  • What serializes. The two slots' fits run one after the other on the one card's stream. The ring overlaps a batch's copies and kernels with the previous batch's fit; it does not overlap two fits. Expected to cost little, since two solves on one card time-slice. Not measured.
  • Further handles are not built. A step that needs concurrent library calls inside itself declares handles of its own, each with a workspace it budgets. No step here needs it.
  • The ring is part of the layout. A slot's device and pinned bytes are tenants of eri's scratch, so the layout holds two of each, and the width is the widest whose ring fits the step's limit, as that experiment sizes it.
  • A simple library user needs none of this. The orthogonalizer and the SCF put everything on the card's stream, in the order they enqueue, with no event.

How it maps onto this design

The sizes below predate 2026-11-23. They take B as one 7.80 GB slab on device 0, built whole, with the whole cells work list beside it. As now decided (batched only, shared code), B's slab is on the host heap, the cards hold one batch of pairs each as scratch, and the work lists are a batch's. The graph, the phases and the O(N²) charges stand; the device total, the two budget figures and the node table's gly10 column for the three-centre step are to be redone at a batch width.

  • The top three levels are the setup phase. The reads, the electron count, the nuclear repulsion and the two basis sets are small host work, unbudgeted, and they are what fills n_atom, n_ao, n_aux and n_occ. Budget mode starts below them.
  • Everything below is a step, each with a mem_budget that is a function of those dimensions alone.
  • The integrals level is where step-owned streams pay. S, T, V, (μν|P) and (P|Q) have no edges between them. Each enqueues on a stream of its own, so those that call no library overlap on the card with no lane named and no event between them.
  • Three nodes use the library: X (an eigensolve), H (a sum) and B (a triangular solve). Each holds gpu0's lock for its run (one handle set per card). They are on separate chains, so the lock may be contended here: X, or H, waits for B's step; nothing is wrong when it does.
  • The scratch slab is packed: X's eigensolve work, and B's build intermediates with its records and work lists, are unordered, so their regions are disjoint, though X waits on gpu0's lock while the three-centre step holds it; the SCF, which follows both, may reuse either. The slab is allocated once, by the final budget node, beside the fixed 128 MiB BLAS-handle workspace.
  • n_pair is budgeted at its bound. The screening map decides how many pairs (μν|P) and B hold, it is a GPU computation and not a parse, and it changes from one geometry to the next, while the unscreened count does not. So both are budgeted at the unscreened count, which also keeps a replay inside its buffers. That wastes memory on a large sparse molecule. Kept on 2026-11-24, with B on the host heap: batching bounds what a card holds, not B's slab, so the waste is host memory and there is no remedy for it short of a tighter bound, which is not taken; a replay can then never outgrow its buffers. The bound is the count in the tree's own pair layout and not the bare n_ao(n_ao+1)/2: gly10 below holds 270,497 pair columns where that formula gives 269,745. decided
  • The step boundary around B decides the peak. As two steps, (μν|P) and B are two slabs of n_pair × n_aux, both alive for the run. The solve is in place, so as one step ("three-centre integrals, then the solve") the Output is one slab. At the experiment's gly10 in cc-pVDZ / cc-pVDZ-JKFIT (n_ao 734, n_aux 3606, 270,497 pair columns) one slab is 7.8 GB, so the choice is 7.8 or 15.6 GB. The metric and its factor are 104 MB each, and every n_ao² matrix 4.3 MB. decided 2026-11-21: one step. (μν|P) is built and solved within it; built into B's own slab, the in-place solve needs no second one, and the build's other intermediates go in step scratch. The batched and multi-GPU paths will change these sizes.
  • The whole example's budget, on that molecule, is then 9.86 GB of device memory: the one slab (7.80), the records and work lists (1.81, nearly all of it the three-centre step's cells), the metric and its factor (0.21), and 0.04 for everything else, the eigensolve's scratch included; plus the 128 MiB handle workspace, and the records and work lists' 1.81 GB pinned. An earlier draft said "the slab plus about 0.25 GB": it left the work lists out. Packing does not lower this: the peak is inside the three-centre step, where its slab, its work lists and its build intermediates are all live at once. Packing frees the 1.81 GB, on each side, only for the steps after it. The work lists are the second-largest charge, and cells is a dense cross product a kernel could derive from two short lists (to measure). The metric and its factor were named here as the other case, Outputs only B reads, which packing Outputs would cover; the derivative steps read them too (shared code), so they stay for the whole calculation by right.

What the graph says

  • Three independent roots. The geometry, the orbital basis and the auxiliary basis are read with no ordering between them.
  • Each basis has its own side. The orbital set feeds its transform and the pair screening map; the auxiliary set feeds its own transform. The two sides meet only at (μν|P).
  • The widest level is the integrals. S, T, V, (μν|P) and (P|Q) depend on nothing in their own level, so all five can run at once. That level is also where nearly all the cost and memory are: (μν|P) dominates both.
  • Two chains hang off it. S → X on the orbital side, and (P|Q) → factor → B on the fitting side. X and the factorization are independent of each other and of T and V.
  • The initial guess is off to one side. It is a table read and an assembly that needs only the orbital basis set: no integral, no S, no H, no X. It can run beside everything after the basis set is built.
  • B is the only node that joins the two bases' heavy products. It is the last to finish, and (μν|P) and the factor are not needed once it has.
  • The geometry reaches the SCF five ways: through the shell centres, through which elements get an atomic density (by way of the basis set), through V's point charges, through the nuclear repulsion, and through the electron count.

Choices the graph leaves open

  • How (P|Q) is "orthogonalized". A Cholesky factor J = LLᵀ with a triangular solve, or a symmetric J−1/2 from an eigendecomposition, which can also drop near-dependent fitting functions. Either gives a B with (μν|λσ) ≈ ΣP BμνP BλσP.
  • How X is built. Symmetric S−1/2, or canonical orthogonalization that drops small eigenvalues of S and so may give fewer orbitals than basis functions.
  • Where the spherical transform is applied. Inside each integral batch, as drawn, or once on a finished Cartesian matrix. Applied inside, no Cartesian-sized (μν|P) is ever stored.
  • Which initial guess. Drawn as a superposition of atomic densities read from a table computed ahead of time, one entry per element and basis. Computing them in the run instead adds a small SCF per element ahead of this node. A core-Hamiltonian guess would instead depend on H, X and the electron count.
  • What the screening map bounds. Drawn as the Schwarz bound over orbital shell pairs. An overlap or distance criterion gives the same node with a cheaper body; a bound that also uses √(P|P) would add an edge from the auxiliary set.
  • Whether B is stored at all. It can be held whole, held in μν-packed form, or rebuilt in slices each iteration from (μν|P) and the factor; the graph up to the factor is the same.

Left out on purpose

  • Symmetry, frozen orbitals, effective core potentials. None changes the shape; an ECP adds one more term beside V.

Failure, diagnostics, determinism

  • A plan over budget is a refusal, not a panic: the final budget node prints the table and exits with an error. An allocation the counter admitted and the device then refused aborts at the failing call, by the tree's create policy, still at init. A cap the machine cannot meet is not that case: every cap is clamped to what the machine has at init (caps), so it is a refusal with the table.
  • A launch that fails for memory reports what it can read. By execute mode everything budgeted is allocated, so a kernel launch that returns an allocation error is about memory outside the budget (what a card takes after budget mode). The error names the step and the kernel, prints the card's free memory and, on CUDA, the device's stack limit, and lists what can cause it on that backend: on CUDA the kernel's stack, when its frame is larger than any launched so far; modules loading at a first launch; another process on the card. It suggests a lower device cap, or looking for the other process. It does not guess which, and cannot print the kernel's own frame, which would need the manifest that is not kept. decided 2026-11-24
  • A config whose steps do not close is refused when the DAG is built: a step that declares it reads another the config does not ask for, named with what it needs.
  • A step's failure in execute mode is a Result. A failed step fails its successors and nothing else, so the wrapper holds a failed flag per node and skips a node with a failed predecessor. rustflow catches a panic per task and surfaces it from wait as RunError::Panicked (README); that replaces the unwinding guard programming_model.md describes. Built in the experiment, not tested.
  • The budget table is the memory diagnostic: one row per step and kind, each step's scratch and the handle workspace their own rows, the packed scratch peak beside the plain sum of the declarations (so packing's gain reads plainly) and the lower bound with the steps that make it (so its distance from the best reads plainly), the totals against the caps, each cap as written beside the value used when it was clamped. Logged on every run, printed in full on a refusal. rustflow's ChromeObserver and dump() (Graphviz DOT) sit beside the timing record.
  • Determinism. Results are those of the edges. Accept or refuse, the scratch offsets, and the table, are the same every run. The order steps were budgeted or timed in is not.

Open questions

Set aside for a fuller discussion

  • Several nodes. The commitment is to a single node's cards (batched only). The further one was raised on 2026-11-23: a step runs on several nodes with several GPUs each, or is not built. Against making it a gate now: nothing here can test it, and a rule with no check drifts. What would keep it open cheaply is one rule, that work on different cards communicates only through the collectives layer; B on one host's heap is the first thing that rule would have to answer. to discuss
  • The step-end sync and the library stream. Closed 2026-11-24: the library stream any step could enqueue on is gone, under one handle set per card and that card's lock. Revised 2026-11-25: a step's library calls go on the card's own stream, which its lock holder has alone and the wrapper syncs (one handle set per card). decided
  • Packing Outputs too, by the same closure, held until the measurement says Outputs are what stacks. Step scratch. to discuss
  • What is cleared between replays, and by whom. A prologue node, an epilogue node, or a reset mode on the steps that need one (an explicit on_replay suggested). Tagged for later. Steps. to discuss

To measure, verify or pick a value for

  • The blocking sync. Which scheduling flag makes a worker waiting on its step's stream sleep, on each backend. If what it costs on the SCF iteration argues for a cheaper replacement, the replacement has to keep pinned scratch safe as well as device scratch (step scratch).
  • The executor's width against the GPU steps that can be waiting at once.
  • What the sum costs. The ratio of summed step peaks to the fold's peak, per examples/ config, split per step into Output bytes and scratch bytes, and whether the peak sits inside one step or across many. That says whether packing scratch is enough, whether Outputs should be packed too (the same closure), or whether the remedy is a step's batched path. Not measured; information only, no bar.
  • Packing quality. The packed peak against the lower bound the closure gives (the heaviest set of mutually unordered declarations), on the same configs. On the model's dummy and random DAGs it is answered (there): at the bound on 98.1% largest first, the order taken; real configs remain, and the table's lower-bound row answers it on each.
  • The rest of the workspace sweep. Not measured: upper solves, rectangular syrk and gemv, the batched solver calls, hipBLASLt. What the sentinel tests would run, if taken (workspace, proposed).
  • The host and device margins' values are picked (caps): judgment, to be raised when a launch fails for memory or a run is killed on the host. The kernels' stack is not read per card.
  • Scratch on HIP while a kernel runs. The measurement that ROCm reserves nothing for a kernel's scratch read the card after each launch, on ROCm 7.2.4 (what a card takes after budget mode). To rerun on ROCm 10 with the card's memory sampled during a large launch of a large-frame kernel: what it takes then is the HIP share of the device margin.
  • The default limit fraction that picks a step's batch width.
  • Task granularity inside a step. A step is one node of the outer graph (decided); how much of its inner graph is rustflow's (a subflow, a pipeline) and how much stays a plain loop on one stream. A chain of small launches belongs in one task.
  • The cells work list on the device. It is a dense cross product of orbital shell pairs and aux shells, so a kernel could derive each entry from two short lists (58,653 and 1,272 entries on gly10) and drop 1.79 GB pinned and 1.79 GB device. A kernel change, in FelizJueves; what it costs in the kernel is to measure. Unlike packing, it lowers DF-HF's peak: the work lists are live inside the step that sets it. Lengths.
  • Rebuilding the records and work lists on a replay. Every run rebuilds them in place, since the records hold coordinates. Whether rebuilding only the records is worth a second path depends on what the full rebuild costs per geometry step.
  • Collectives. An all-GPU step holds a stream per card, so one rank's calls within a step stay on one stream. Whether NCCL and RCCL mind a rank moving between steps' streams is to verify.
  • Idle workers. rustflow's workers give up a search after a fixed number of attempts and park through a notifier (DESIGN.md, from a summary; verify in src/executor/notifier.rs). What that costs while a run waits on the device needs reading.
  • Submitting from outside the pool. "A graph submitted from outside the pool reaches it only by stealing", about 100 ns a task (README). At a few dozen steps it should not matter.
  • Pinned pages and NUMA. With no thread per device, a pinned slab is allocated and touched by whichever worker runs that step's mem_budget.
  • The large-allocation check and C libraries. The allocator wrapper does not see a library's own malloc.
  • The dependency. Settled: rustflow is unpublished, so it is a git dependency pinned once, by its full commit (f184bcab…, its head on 2026-11-24), in rust/Cargo.toml's [workspace.dependencies]; a member inherits it with workspace = true, and a prototype under experimental/rust/ names the same rev (v0.0.2 builds at it). The rule is in rust/docs/testing.md, "What runs when", and doc_check/tests/git_dependencies.rs checks it. Cargo locks a workspace dependency only once a member uses it, so rust/Cargo.lock has no rustflow entry yet. to discuss: rustflow depends on crossbeam-deque, which doc_check's thread-pool rule (rust/docs/programming_model.md, "CPU workers") forbids any member to reach. The first member of rust/ on rustflow, the executor's port or a prototype if one joins the workspace, changes that rule with it; a prototype that is a workspace of its own, as v0.0.2 and v0.0.3 are and v0.0.4 will be, needs no change and locks the pin's rev.
  • Deferred by decision: AVAS, the semi-numerical Hessian, semi-numerical gradients.
