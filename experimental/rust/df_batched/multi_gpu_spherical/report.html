B over two cards

../single_gpu_spherical builds the density-fitting factor B on one card. Here each card builds a share of every class's shell pairs. Two cards build B 1.93× to 1.99× faster than one, from gly10 (2.8 GB) to gly40 (46.7 GB), and the two-card B matches the one-card B to 1.5e-13.

How it is split

B[Q][μν] = Σ_P L⁻¹[Q][P] (P|μν),   M = (P|Q) = L Lᵀ

Once L exists each column of B is independent, so the cards split the columns and nothing is reduced. A card's trips are ../single_gpu_spherical's, unchanged: the ssperi3 integrals, the trsm against L, the transpose and the pitched download.

  • Each card takes a contiguous share of every class's pairs, by pair count. The trsm is most of a trip and costs the same per column, and a class's pairs all have the same block, so equal pairs is equal work. Splitting whole classes between cards would not balance: their costs differ widely.
  • Each card writes a heap row of its own. The planner orders every writer of a row after the one before it, and the arena gives a row one writer at a time, so two cards downloading into one B would take turns. A card's row holds its columns alone; Part::spans says where they sit in the pair map's layout, and the driver puts the parts back for the check.
  • Every card's row is made before any loop. The first version made card 1's row after card 0's loops. The heap laid it out in card 0's finished bytes, which ordered card 1's whole build after card 0's: two cards took 5.2 s on gly10 against one card's 4.4 s, each card showing half the device time. With the rows made first it is 2.27 s.
  • Each card builds its own metric and L. A row crosses between workers by a collective alone, and none is used here. The prefix is the same wall on two cards as on one, but it grows faster than the build: 26.9 s at gly40, 30% of the two-card build.
  • Each card plans its own width, an adaptive part on its own worker in its own room.

Correctness: two cards against one

--check keeps each run's B, puts the two-card parts back in the map's columns, and compares element by element with the one-card B built in the same process. One card is ../single_gpu_spherical's build, which that experiment checked against rust/'s add_packed_b.

The assembled B starts as NaN, so "unset" counts any element no part wrote. The integrals are the same on both paths; the trsm sees a different column partition, which is where gly10's roundoff comes from. Why gly20 is bitwise and gly10 is not was not looked into.

The trsm is cuBLAS's dtrsm, naux² operations per column, so it grows as N³ while the integrals and the copies grow as N²: 62% of gly10's build, 87% of gly40's. On two cards every column of this table is the same in total and half per card.

The host stages are not hidden. At a full card's room each class is one trip, and the classes run one after another, so no trip's unstage has a next trip's compute to run under. gly10's build is its card time (3.82 s) plus its two CPU stages (0.57 s). That share falls as the trsm grows: 13% at gly10, 4% at gly40.

More trips let the ring overlap a trip's unstage with the next trip's compute, which recovers 2.6% of the 4% the host stages cost. At 4 GB the batches are narrow enough that the trsm itself slows by 18%. So the room is not worth tuning for speed, and the build runs at full speed in 8 GB per card.

One run per row after a warm-up; the run-to-run spread at gly40 was not measured, so the first three rows may be within it. A 2 GB row was started and stopped.

What is here, and what is not

file
src/ssperi3.rsadd_packed_b_parts: ../single_gpu_spherical's add_packed_b_spherical, forked to build each worker's share into its own heap row. The cells, the staging, the fit and the download are that file's.
src/main.rsdf-batched-multi-gpu: the prefix on every card, the parts, --cards (by default 1 and then every card), --check, and a per-card, per-task timing breakdown.
build.rsLinks ../single_gpu_spherical's libfbx_ssperi3_kernels.so; no kernels are built here.
  • J and K are not here. They split over the aux index, not over pairs: K = 2 Σ_Q (B_Q C)(B_Q C)ᵀ is a sum of independent terms per Q, and couples pairs that sit on different cards. So B changes hands through the host between the build and J/K, and a J/K step will read these per-card rows or a merged B.
  • L is not broadcast. One metric and Cholesky and a collective to the other cards would take the duplicate prefix off them.
  • Classes do not overlap. Letting a class's integrals start under the previous class's last unstage would hide the host stages at any room.
